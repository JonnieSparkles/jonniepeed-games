# Unruggabull II

Built from [spec.md](spec.md); why things are the way they are, and what was tried and dropped, is in [history.md](history.md). The game is at `site/unruggabull-ii/index.html`. This build has Floor 13 and the roof framework with stand-in drawing. Start plays Floor 13; `#stage=roof&seed=42` plays the roof directly. The floors between them and the ending after dawn are still planned. It's unlisted: no card on either shelf, only its direct link, and it keeps `<meta name="robots" content="noindex">` and has local best scores only. The original game, Unruggabull: RugCo Alley, stays in its own repo.

## Finding and promoting the demo

Go to `unruggabull-ii/` directly; nothing on the studio page links to it. To list it, add a card to `site/index.html` (on Side B, a `data-side="b" data-badge="demo" hidden` card like Stick Army's; the thumbnail is `unruggabull-ii/thumb.webp`) and update the studio harness's card counts. To promote it after approval, give it a Side A card, remove the noindex tag, update this doc and the README, run the checks below, stamp last and publish by hand.

## Floor 13 rules

- **Title and story.** The title screen is RugCo Tower at sunset, souls drifting up to p(Loom)'s beacon. The first Start on a device tells the story so far over it, typed out in narrator blips: a tap shows the whole line, another moves on, Skip or Escape ends it. After that, Start goes straight to the floor and the title card has a **Watch the story** link.
- **Controls.** A thumb stick on the left and Shoot and Slash on the right. Hold Shoot to keep firing (five shots a second) or tap for one; shots lean toward the nearest temp or carpshit in a cone ahead. Like the first game, the blaster runs on charges: 20, shown under the hearts, with one back every half second. Run dry and it powers down with an "EMPTY" pop, clicks and flashes red when you try, and only fires as charges return; a chirp says it's full again. Keys: A/D or arrows to move, W, Up or Space to jump, S, C or Down to crouch, Shift or ' to shoot (J or Z too), Enter to slash (K or X too). Keys are read by position, so holding Shift doesn't change them. M sound, F full screen, P or Escape pause. On touch screens, Start goes full screen and the controls appear under the scene (in landscape, in the bottom corners). The stick has no frame, just a faint ring where it rests, and it floats: put your thumb down anywhere in its area and that's the centre (a hint under it shows until the first touch). Slide left or right to move, flick up to jump (once per flick; come back toward the middle to flick again), hold down to crouch. If the thumb wanders well past the ring, the centre follows it. Shoot and Slash are round arcade buttons, blue with a pixel blaster and red with a pixel katana, that sink and glow while held. The ring round Shoot is the blaster's charge (red when low, grey while the blaster is down); the ring round Slash sweeps back as it's ready again. In portrait the scene sits right on top of the stick's area and Shoot over Slash, and the title card says it's best played in landscape; in landscape Slash sits a little higher than Shoot. A finger can slide between Shoot and Slash, and a touch in the gap between them counts for the nearer one.
- **Crouch.** Held on the ground only: half height, so anything at head height passes over, but no moving or shooting (slashing still works); on touch screens Shoot greys out while you're down. Jumping from a crouch works. Standing on a pulling runner, crouching grips it: it drags you at a quarter of the speed ("GRIP!").
- **Slash** cuts carpshits in two, knocks paper wads, bundles and staples back where they came from (a deflect, which also gives the blaster 3 charges back and flashes the charge bar), and on a pulling runner cuts the rug. Shots go straight through paper, so slashing it back or dodging are the only answers. Temps are safe from the katana behind their desks: the blaster or their own paper gets them.
- **Unruggabull talks**: about seventeen short lines in a small caption at the top left, under the hearts, each once per run (the floor's start, the first deflect, moving day ("LIFT WITH YOUR LEGS."), lights out, the first Spread Shot and coffee, a streak, the copy room, an answer to the Shredder's opening line, the first jam and smash, one heart left, the clear, and "WHOA!" when he's rugged, "HEY! THAT WAS MINE." when the surge takes his blaster, and "CLEAN SWEEP." for his first formation wipe). Long lines wrap into short ones, so the caption stays at the left, clear of the signs hanging over the hall. He talks in his own blips for now; the lines in `LINES` in `game.js` are the recording script for Jonnie's voice. He never talks over the Shredder.
- **Unruggabull** swings his arms as he runs (with a little bob), throws them up to jump, raises the blaster beside his head to shoot (bolts and the muzzle flash come from there, with a kick), swings the katana from over his shoulder, and flinches when hit. He jumps a fifth of the hall high for 0.65 seconds, stretching on takeoff and squashing on landing, with a shadow that shrinks as he rises; at the top he clears low paper and carpshits. Landing kicks up dust. Hit, he staggers a step back toward you, rocking side to side (drawn only). Standing about, he breathes; after 2 seconds he glances left and right; after 5 he does something, in turn: spins the blaster round his finger, checks the katana in its sheath, scuffs a hoof. Moving, shooting, slashing, jumping or crouching resets it. At the clear he throws the horns up.
- **Incoming paper** has a jagged edge that blinks red and orange (never a round glow, so it can't pass for a pickup) and is drawn a little bigger than paper you knocked back. The Shredder's bundles are wads of shredded paper, strips sticking out every way, that spin and shed strips behind them. Nothing marks the floor. Flying carpshits ripple as they fly.
- **Carpshits at work** line the walls from far down the hall, about one every 6 to 8 seconds, each section with its own mix (`work` in `BEATS`): Accounts Payable and All Staff have them typing (half of those in an accountant's green visor), on a call in a headset with a chart of Q4 rug targets going down behind them, asleep at the desk (Zs, drool, the monitor on its screensaver), two gossiping at the water cooler, and a manager pacing on the phone in an office set into the wall (a real room behind a window, seen at a changing angle as you pass). The Copy Room has them copying themselves (lying on the copier's glass while copies pile up), typing and asleep. They never fight back. In reach, the blaster leans to them like temps, and a shot frees the soul inside (one per carpshit; the cooler has two), counting double in the dark, where their eyes glow; a shot through the office's glass cracks it. Stations leave them room: none on that side right where one sits.
- **Pickups** float at jump height, so you jump to grab them, and are made to stand out: a beam of light from the ceiling, a glow on the floor, a halo, an outline that flashes white, sparkles going round, and a twinkle when one turns up. A RugCo coffee gives a heart back (never past five). A Spread Shot fills the blaster and fires three bolts at a time for 10 seconds. A coffee and a Spread Shot come after moving day, a coffee after lights out; temps drop a coffee one time in five when you're hurt; the Shredder drifts one in when the fight starts and at each phase change, if you're hurt.
- **Rows** span the whole aisle, so the only way past is a jump: rolling office chairs packed side by side in All Staff, with caution tape along their backs so there's plainly no way through (high backs, five-star bases, spinning casters and a swivel wobble; they tip over if one hits you) and a paper jam's sheet in the Copy Room (a wall-to-wall printed page curling up at you, its corner flapping), every 7 to 10 seconds. The first section has none, so jumping isn't needed straight away. The first row shows a "JUMP" prompt and the first pickup "JUMP TO GRAB".
- **Words on screen** are kept few. Prompts during play are one short line in a small strip at the top, never across the hall: JUMP, DUCK, JUMP TO GRAB, STEP OFF OR SLASH, SLASH THE RUG, RIDE THE RUG, SLASH IT BACK, WIN A RALLY FOR IT, each once. A deflect pops just its charges ("+3"); kills in the dark don't pop "×2" (the meter shows it).
- **Feel.** Only one "EMPTY" shows at a time, and a pop with the same words close by isn't repeated. Kills pause the action for a few frames, blaster hits throw sparks, and souls freed in a quick streak ring a step higher each time. Each freed soul floats up, then flies into the souls counter, which pulses as it lands (the count shown catches up as they land). At one heart the screen edges pulse red with a heartbeat. Now and then a desk phone rings down the hall, and a copier whirs in the copy room. A streak of 10 in the hall drops a Spread Shot just ahead of you ("10 STREAK!"), once per section or event. Wipe out every carpshit in a formation before any of it is halfway down the hall and it pays a bonus soul for each, with a "WIPED OUT +5" pop, a chime and sparks; in lights out it's a big moment instead (a big "WIPED OUT! +5", a fanfare and a beat of hit-stop).
- **The goal.** The floor opens with "FLOOR 13" then "FREE 90 SOULS", 1.6 seconds each, over the ceiling with no band across the hall, so the hall itself is the reveal, and a meter at the top fills toward it. Every kill frees one soul; the score is souls freed.
- **The hall** comes in three beats, each announced by a sign that drops from the ceiling (the events between them get signs too, never a banner across the hall):
  The house lights are up in the first sections (Accounts Payable, moving day, All Staff), an ordinary office, and ease down into the copy room, which stays moody, like the dark and the fight. Blaster bolts light the hall softly as they fly, so steady fire doesn't strobe it.
  Each section dresses the walls its own way, with a chair rail in its colour (blue, red, beige) and a ceiling light that flickers now and then: Accounts Payable has framed motivational posters (a mountain, a cat, a graph, a rug), clocks, windows onto the city at night, office doors, plants and water coolers; All Staff has streamers, an ALL STAFF banner, balloons, windows and plants; the Copy Room has paper stacks and shelves of reams, recycling bins, a vending machine, a notice board and clocks. All of it is cosmetic.
  1. **Accounts Payable** (26 seconds, RugCo Tower): cubicles with temps (three looks: a red tie; glasses and a blue tie; a comb-over and a teal tie, picked by the desk) who pop up and sometimes (60%) throw a paper wad, leading you a little if you're moving (a temp about to throw flashes a "!" card first); single carpshits that chase you; file boxes from 16 seconds, to jump or step around. Posters and water coolers on the walls.
  2. **Moving day** (14 seconds, a hazard-yellow sign "MOVING DAY"): a short course that introduces jumping and ducking. Carpshits clear out and the temps stay down, but the blaster stays on. Facilities has left furniture down the middle of the hall, in a set order with set gaps (`MOVES` in `floor13.js`): a row of chairs to jump, a plank across two stepladders to duck, then one more of each. Nothing rolls, so pieces keep their spacing. The first jump and the first duck say so (JUMP rides the chairs, DUCK rides the plank). The stepladders stand on the floor so you can see where the plank is; its underside is well above his head crouched (his crouch hunches him over, head down) and level with his face standing, and it carries on over him as he passes, with a "DUCKED!". Between pieces a soul trapped in a bubble floats in one of three lanes, a different one each time, so you move side to side too: run through it to free it. Running into furniture costs a heart, like a box in the hall. Each piece you get past cleanly frees a soul; get through without a bump and it's "NOT A SCRATCH +10".
  3. **All Staff** (20 seconds, Alley Redux): carpshits arrive in formations (a V, a line with one gap that comes in at head height, so duck it or find the gap, and a snake), one hit each, and rows of office chairs roll down the aisle. Red streamers on the walls. No new formation starts in the last 5 seconds, and the lights wait (up to 8 seconds) for the last one to clear.
  4. **Lights out** (10 seconds, bass and a heartbeat, a neon sign "LIGHTS OUT ×2"): the ceiling panels go out, and only monitors, eyes, paper, pickups and the runner's edges show, with you, rows and boxes faint. Blaster bolts are lights: they light the hall around them as they fly and show in the polished floor, and the muzzle flash lights you. A snake comes down the hall, then a formation every 2.2 seconds (a V, a high line, a snake) and single carpshits every second or so (one hit each). The temps stay down. Every soul freed in the dark counts double. Every 3 seconds a light switch glows on one wall; shoot it (move toward that wall and your shots lean to it) and the lights flash on for 1.4 seconds, freezing every carpshit in the hall while they still pay double. Far down the endless hall, two red eyes open now and then, watching.
  5. **Copy Room** (the Tower tune up a key): temps behind copiers, file boxes, paper sheets, and the runner rug. It ends when you reach the goal (after at least 18 seconds) or after 60 seconds regardless.
- **Far down the hall.** Scenery (wall dressing and the carpshits at work) comes into view far down the hall, out of the fog. What you fight (temps, carpshits, rows, boxes, pickups) still arrives where the far wall used to be and fades in out of the dark there, and only counts as a target for the blaster once it's fully out of the dark (`TUNE.reach`); nothing is drawn behind the Shredder; starting it further back would change how long it takes to reach you, so it waits for a retune.
- **The endless hall.** Until the copy room the hall has no end: it runs on into the fog, ceiling lights and runner and all. In the copy room the Shredder appears far down it and closes in as you near the goal (souls and the room's time both count), reaching the end of the hall as it wakes.
- **The runner.** In the copy room and the boss fight, the red runner warns (its edges flash) and then pulls toward the Shredder. Standing on it drags you deeper; step off, jump, slash to cut it, or crouch to grip it. The first pull shows a prompt ("STEP OFF OR SLASH"). Riding it all the way into the mouth costs two hearts and spits you back out.
- **The Shredder** wakes at the end of the copy room: the hall stops, its eyes light, a card names it ("THE SHREDDER, HEAD OF DOCUMENT DESTRUCTION") while its health bar ticks up to full, it says its line, and the fight starts. Health 100. Blaster hits 0.3; a deflected bundle 6; a deflected staple or paper airplane 2.
  - **Paper airplanes**, in every phase's cycle: a flight of five spreads across the hall at head height (1.6 seconds to reach you), one aimed at you and the others 0.3 apart, so sidestepping lands you in the next one. Duck under them, or slash them back. The first thing at head height (a plane, a high line or a beam) shows "DUCK".
  - Its mouth glows red with a growl 0.45 seconds before every attack. It wears down as you hurt it: up to eight cracks spread across its panels, it smokes past 30% damage, and sparks fly from the cracks past 60%.
  - **It's alive.** Its body is a spring, drawn on its own canvas and scaled with hard pixels about the foot of its mouth: it breathes (faster in phase 3), rears back as its mouth glows, lunges at you as it spits, recoils from deflects and smashes (a smash also sets it shuddering), shudders while jammed, and roars forward into each new phase. Its pupils follow you across the hall, it blinks now and then, and from phase 2 (or while winding up) its brows come down. When you miss a return or it hits you, it lunges forward laughing, its eyes squeezed into arcs and its teeth chattering. Shredded strips drop from its mouth onto the rug while it chews, faster during a pull. Below 15 it flickers and jitters and says "I AM NOT... OUT OF ORDER." once.
  - **It talks back**, never more than once every 12 seconds and never over another line or a banner, each list in turn: when you miss a return ("SHREDDED.", "DENIED.", "FILED UNDER NOPE."), when its paper hits you ("PAPER CUT.", "SIGN HERE. AND HERE.", "THAT GOES ON YOUR RECORD."), and when you smash it ("MY ROLLERS!", "PC LOAD LETTER?!", "THAT VOIDS MY WARRANTY."). Not on the hit that rugs you: his "WHOA!" cuts off whatever it's saying.
  - The runner pulls on a steady beat in every phase, and cutting the rug in any phase jams it (no attacks, no pulls, triple blaster damage) for 2.6 seconds, up to twice that if you ride the rug in close to its mouth before you cut ("BIG JAM!"). Paper you knock back from close to the mouth hits harder too, up to double. Ride all the way in and you still lose two hearts.
  - From phase 2, every other pull is a side spray: the floor beside the rug flashes red while it warns, then staples stream down both sides (not the rug) while it pulls. Ride the rug (and cut it when you like), or jump the staples at the side. The first shows "RIDE THE RUG". No other attacks during a spray.
  - **Rallies**, in every phase: it serves a white-hot bundle and bats your returns back, each one quicker (1 second to start, 0.85 times as long each return, never under 0.45; your returns fly back quicker too), then misses: a smash for 6 + 2 per bat-back, and it reels for 1.6 seconds. It bats back 1 to 2 returns in phase 1, 2 to 4 in phase 2 and 3 to 5 in phase 3. Once a phase it's a **marathon** ("MARATHON!", and the first time "LET'S GO THE DISTANCE."): 6 returns in phase 1, 7 in phase 2, 8 in phase 3, finishing in a bigger smash ("MARATHON SMASH!", a white flash, a freeze and an explosion). A rally builds as it goes: a counter at the top ("RALLY x3", doubling in size from 4, flashing with each return), speed streaks round the bundle, a rising ping, harder shakes, and the music speeding up (up to 1.4 times), settling back when it ends. Miss a return and the rally ends with a heart lost. No attacks or pulls while a rally is on, and rallies wait for a pull to end. The first serve shows "SLASH IT BACK" and the first bat-back gets "RETURN TO SENDER." A slash reaches 0.19 of the hall ahead and knocks paper back for 0.2 seconds after you press it; each slash draws a white swoosh arc round him (a short one on the wind-up, the full sweep on the cut), and a deflect bursts where the blade meets the paper.
  - Phase 1 (above 66): an attack every 1.4 seconds, cycling a rally, a bundle (2.2 seconds to reach you), a volley of three scraps of shredded paper 0.24 seconds apart (1.4 seconds each, worth 3 knocked back) for tap-tap deflects, the marathon, and paper airplanes. It keeps attacking while the runner pulls (but not while it warns). Pulls of 2.4 seconds, 6 seconds apart.
  - Phase 2 (above 33): an attack every 1.5 seconds, cycling a staple fan (four with one gap), the marathon, a quicker bundle (1.6 seconds), paper airplanes, a rally, a paper jam sheet that skids out of its mouth across the whole floor (jump it), and a volley of four scraps. Pulls of 3.2 seconds, 5.5 apart.
  - Into phase 3 it says "ENTERING POWER SAVING MODE." and the lights go out for 7 seconds: only its eyes, its teeth, the paper and the runner's edges show, and the bolts light the way.
  - Phase 3: the volley match. A second after the lights go out, a power surge you can't dodge flashes blue and knocks the blaster out of his hands, spinning up the rug ("WIN A RALLY FOR IT"). The cause is shown plainly: for 0.9 seconds before every surge its eyes flicker blue and white and crackle with blue sparks (with a growl), then a jagged bolt of blue lightning runs from its eyes to the gun in his hand. It lies on the rug crackling; pulls carry it toward the mouth, and Shoot only clicks. Win a rally and the smash makes the Shredder reel and the rug roll back toward you ("REWIND!", blue edges), carrying the blaster to the front, where it flashes gold and you walk onto it to grab it. The first time you get it back, three souls leave the counter one after another (0.6 seconds apart, 1.4 seconds each, each on its own wide arc, drawn double size with a glow and a sparkle trail) and fly into the gun; the first two each land with one soft bell (D5, then F5) and add a stream, thin and white with a gold halo and tail, beside the bolt (two streams, then three, fanning slightly, never bending in). After four seconds of firing (the hold doesn't run while he isn't shooting) the streams drop off one by one, each one's soul leaving the gun and flying back to the counter, 0.6 seconds apart, without a sound. Nothing announces it (the live region only says "Blaster back."), the counter keeps its souls, and the streams do normal damage for no extra charges. It's the first glimpse of the mega stream, which fuses only on the roof ([spec](spec.md#the-mega-stream)). A rolling rug shoves anyone on it to the back of the hall and pins them there: no sidestepping. It cycles a rally, another surge (a rally instead if the blaster's already gone), the marathon, a rewind (the rug rolls back and a bundle comes at you: pinned on the rug, knock it back), paper airplanes, a rally, a staple fan, a volley of five scraps and a staple carpet, every 1.4 seconds. It braces (blaster hits do half damage unless it's jammed or reeling). No attacks or pulls while a roll is on, and rewinds wait for a pull to end. Pulls of 3.8 seconds, 5 apart.
- **Clear.** The final hit flashes white and freezes for a moment, then 1.3 seconds run at a third of the speed while the Shredder shudders, then it blows and says "RUG NOT FOUND." Thirteen souls come out of it, the souls freed on the floor (up to 40 of them) drift up past him and away, Unruggabull throws the horns up, "FLOOR 13 CLEAR", then a card with souls freed, time, deflects, best streak, formation wipes, smashes, longest rally, continues (if any) and best.
- **Rugged.** Five hearts, 1.7 seconds of safety after each hit. At zero the rug is yanked out from under him (the runner's stripes zip away): he flips up, spins in quarter turns and falls off the bottom of the screen. In the hall, "Rugged. Continue?" starts the floor over. Beaten by the Shredder, Continue picks up at the fight with full hearts and the souls you had when it woke (it says "BACK FOR SECONDS?"), and Restart floor starts over. The clock keeps running across continues, and a clear with continues doesn't set the fastest clear.

**Play stats** are wired up but switched off (`"reporting": false` in `stats/games.json`) until Jonnie turns them on. A run starts at Start (a continue at the Shredder is a run of its own), ends at the clear or rugged card, and a restart from pause reports as a quit. Score is souls freed; stats are `stage` (1–9) and `stage_name`, `cause` (what took the last heart, or `cleared`), `continues`, `boss_hp`, `smashes`, `best_rally`, `wipes` and `deflects`. `stats/test/games.py` checks it.

Best souls and fastest clear are kept in `localStorage` (`unruggabull-ii-best`), whether the story has been seen in `unruggabull-ii-story`, and sound mute in `unruggabull-ii-muted`.

## Roof rules (Part A)

The approved [roof plan](roof-plan.md) and [storyboard](roof-storyboard.png) are implemented from elevator to dawn. Motion, light, audio and the music system are built; final art, music composition and phone tuning remain in Part B; current drawing uses shapes, existing sprites and the game font. `unruggabull-ii/#stage=roof` starts it directly; combine it with `&seed=42` to replay. Normal Start still plays Floor 13, whose clear does not advance to the roof. Roof bests use a separate local key.

- **Lead-in:** doors open over four seconds, then control and eight seconds of slow walking past units and a tank. One white frame reveals the loom, with half a second of raised ambient light. Its eye opens, rides to the middle, and the P(LOOM) card appears. Its orange readout types “HELLO, UNRUGGABULL.”, “I HAVE MODELED THIS MEETING.” and “PROBABILITY OF YOUR SURVIVAL: 1%.” The bar fills to 99%, then FIGHT and the roof music hook start round 1.
- **Three spools:** each round breaks one spool. The odds stay at 99% throughout, flashing with “NO CHANGE.” at every snap. Volleys arrive every six seconds. Once the next paced opening is due, completing a volley stalls the eye white-hot for 2.3 seconds; only blaster hits during that window hurt its spool (10 hits each). Earlier volleys leave it closed. The eye follows the player between openings. Nominal 30/40/45-second rounds space two openings per round; damage ends the round, so a missed opening extends it rather than adding health.
- **Caught souls:** carpshits fly from the cloth in groups of 2/3/4, every 2.8/2.5/2 seconds. Groups keep their spacing using the existing formation movement; each kill counts a soul and its wisp flies into the warp with CAUGHT. A roof-only run starts with `ROOF.initialCaught = 100` earlier-floor captives, already in the counter and faintly visible during the walk. All captives are drawn in six rows of ten thread gaps, with shallow overlapping layers: about 60 distinct silhouettes at the quiet and a counter in the 200s. Every captive gets its own return flight; only simultaneous flight drawing is capped (96), never counts. Dawn raises the same captured population, without scoring it twice.
- **Predictions:** three dotted rings sit at depth 0.28, further up the roof with percentage labels above them. Their percentages add to 100 (75/21/4, then 81/16/3 and 90/9/1). The likely ring is nearest the player when the tell begins; the other two swap using the seeded stream. The first orb always lands on the likely ring; two more choose by the displayed probabilities. A low-odds hit says “VARIANCE.” Walk up the roof into a less likely ring or jump the impact. Up/W and Down/S/C, or up/down on the stick, move forward/back in a 0–0.36 depth band at 0.45 units a second; left/right still sidestep and Space still jumps. Letting go holds his depth. These controls are roof-only. Charges refill every 0.35 seconds here to support the larger groups; Floor 13 keeps 0.5 seconds.
- **Rounds 2 and 3:** a snare targets his current lane, hooks, then reels him toward the loom. Slash cuts it, pops CUT and flinches the eye for a second with “RECALCULATING.” Reaching the end costs a heart and throws him back. Rain leans for 0.8 seconds before a gust pushes him sideways; the low parapet clamps his lane. Round 3 adds these attacks more frequently.
- **Quiet and return:** after spool 3, “ALL SOULS ACCOUNTED FOR.” finishes, music cuts, and two quiet seconds pass with movement but no threats. The first-soul cut lasts a second. Three souls land at the gun with `play('bell', 0/1/2)` (D5/F5/A5), then the rest flood back, streams bend together and fuse. From the first return, charges are gold and unlimited. The readout stutters “ERROR. ERR.”
- **Beam:** fusion flashes and freezes for 0.14 seconds, then the beam cut holds for 0.65 seconds. Back behind him, “THAT OUTCOME WAS NOT IN THE MODEL.” gets his caption “LET'S DISCUSS THOSE ODDS.” Left/right steer the continuous thick beam. It drains 10 percentage points a second while overlapping the eye; off it, odds recover at three points a second. It melts carpshits in its lane; a hit costs a heart and leaves the beam running. After an initial 2.5 seconds the eye races along its rail, faster as odds fall, saying “PROBABILITY OF CAPTURE: ZERO.” and “RECALCULATING.” when caught again.
- **Finish:** at 1%, “UNLIKELY.” holds for a beat. The snap cut lasts 1.5 seconds; the eye goes dark, rain stops, dawn opens and the chord hook plays. The full captured population drifts upward before the Roof clear card; all those souls are already counted. The subsequent story is outside this build.
- **Five hearts and continues:** death in a round restarts that round with full hearts and its spool intact; previously broken spools and caught souls, including wisps still in flight, persist. Death in the crescendo resumes at the quiet, restoring the captured warp and replaying the return. Continuing skips the lead-in. Cut-ins hold held keys and buffered actions and freeze all fight timers; they contain no threats.

The presentation uses existing sprites and shapes. Walk souls are faint glows without outlines, lightning exposes dark silhouettes, and wake brings them up to full. Captures arc into reserved slots and pulse on arrival; the quiet pulses each soul independently. Bell souls shake loose one at a time. Every remaining soul leaves its own slot over one second and accelerates on a curve to the muzzle; the original three-second arrival schedule stays exact. Dawn souls stagger their rises and fade near the top; its displayed counter starts at the pre-dawn total and adds each liberated captive as it fades; the clear card retains this total. Seeded run score stays unchanged.

The eye lights the wet roof and his back orange, angry red during rings/snares, and white during stalls. Two distant flashes precede the reveal; lightning falls off fast. Six gold lights follow the flickering beam, and flying souls add faint warm lights and reflections. Dawn warms from storm blue to orange over two seconds. One decaying shake function covers spool snaps, the building pinch, fusion, the continuous beam and the final snap; reduced motion halves it.

All roof sound uses the existing chip synth and mute: filtered rain/wind from elevator to snap, delayed thunder after each flash, thread whip/cut twang, gust whoosh, spool crack/twang, a sustained filtered saw/square/hiss beam roar (louder on the eye), the final multi-thread crack, and a major chord at dawn. p(Loom) uses lower pulse blips with unchanged caption timing. Three replaceable D-minor candidates have matching heavy versions: chip-metal at 160/180 BPM, synthwave at 126/142 BPM, and epic choir at 140/158 BPM. `#music=a`, `#music=b` or `#music=c` selects one for the actual fight (A is default). The heavy track starts on its downbeat after the fusion freeze. Music cuts immediately after “ALL SOULS ACCOUNTED FOR.”; storm alone remains through the quiet.

## Writing and auditioning music

`music-roof.js` loads before `audio.js`. Each track is one plain object with `bpm`, `key`, `swing` (0–0.45), a named `patterns` map, an `order` list, a zero-based `loop` index into that list, and initial `stems`. Each pattern declares its length in bars (16 steps each) and named channels. Here is a one-bar example:

```js
{
  bpm: 160, key: 'Dm', swing: 0, loop: 0, order: ['A'], stems: ['lead'],
  patterns: { A: { bars: 1, channels: {
    pulse1: { stem: 'lead', wave: 'pulse', duty: .25, vol: .07,
      notes: 'D5 - F5 - A5 - D6 - . . . . . . . .' }
  } } }
}
```

A note name starts a note; `-` holds it and `.` rests for a step. An array can also contain objects such as `{note:'D5', len:2, chord:['F5','A5'], vol:.06, envelope:{attack:.01,decay:.04,sustain:.5,release:.08}, vibrato:{rate:6,depth:.01}, arp:[0,7,12], slide:'A4', echo:{delay:.2,feedback:.25,repeats:2}}`. Length is in steps; envelope, slide duration and echo delay are seconds; arp intervals are semitones (`arpHz` sets their rate); vibrato depth is a fraction of frequency. Channel defaults accept these effects too. Use `wave:'pulse'` with duty `.125`, `.25` or `.5` for either lead, `triangle` for bass, `noise` for drums (`k/s/h`, plus heavy `K/S`, open hat `H`, crash `c`, tom `T`), and `sawtooth` or `square` for a heavy lead. Noise presets combine filtered noise and pitched drops, with a channel/note envelope. Detuned pulse chords supply C's placeholder choir. Echo repeats are capped at four.

Add an object under a new ID in `UnrugRoofMusic`, or call `UnrugSound.registerTrack(id, data)` to audition edited data. An edit to the playing ID replaces it on the next bar, so notes are not cut mid-bar. Update the candidate IDs in the cue selection if adding a fourth direction. `stems([...])` and pattern-track `tempo(rate)` apply at the next scheduled bar, leaving active notes and echoes intact. `music(id,{boundary:'beat'|'bar'})` queues a switch; `music(id,{slam:true})` starts a fresh downbeat immediately. `music(null,{hard:true})` cuts the transport and music overlays; `stinger('spool')` plays a phrase over the music. The original section format and its synthesis remain unchanged.

Roof cues use `roofCue(name, options)`, shared by the game and lab:

| --- | --- |
| Elevator/walk/reveal | None; storm only |
| FIGHT | Selected fight track on its first downbeat, drums/bass/lead |
| Round 2 / round 3 | Add harmony / add power (and C's choir) at the next bar |
