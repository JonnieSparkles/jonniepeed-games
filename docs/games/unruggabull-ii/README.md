# Unruggabull II

Built from [spec.md](spec.md); why things are the way they are, and what was tried and dropped, is in [history.md](history.md). The game is at `site/unruggabull-ii/index.html`. This build joins the whole climb in one run: the stairwell, the 13th Floor, the quiet ride, the 48th Floor and its lobby, the long ride and the roof. Start plays the stairwell, which ends by kicking in the 13th Floor's door; the 13th Floor's clear card has one button, **Continue**, which takes the elevator (the cards are below, under **Cards**). `#stage=stairs` plays the stairwell and `#stage=floor13` the 13th Floor directly; `#stage=floor48&seed=42` plays the 48th Floor directly (`&at=lobby` starts in its lobby); `#stage=elevator&seed=42` plays the long ride directly; `#stage=roof&seed=42` plays the roof directly. After the roof's dawn comes the ending (below, under **The ending**), then the roof's clear card; `#stage=roof&seed=42&at=ending` goes straight to the dawn before it. It's unlisted: no card on either shelf, only its direct link, and it keeps `<meta name="robots" content="noindex">` and has local best scores only. The original game, Unruggabull: RugCo Alley, stays in its own repo.

## Finding and promoting the demo

Go to `unruggabull-ii/` directly; nothing on the studio page links to it. To list it, add a card to `site/index.html` (on Side B, a `data-side="b" data-badge="demo" hidden` card like Stick Army's; the thumbnail is `unruggabull-ii/thumb.webp`) and update the studio harness's card counts. To promote it after approval, give it a Side A card, remove the noindex tag, update this doc and the README, run the checks below, stamp last and publish by hand.

## the 13th Floor rules

- **Title and story.** The title screen ([title-plan.md](title-plan.md), `title.js`) is the first game's view down RugCo Alley a night later: the owner's painting (`title-art.js`, shrunk to 480 × 640 and the game's 64 colours as the intro's stills are), the RugCo Empire garage with its fire out and smoke still coming off the roof, RugCo Tower behind it with p(Loom)'s eye in the pavilion on its roof, and him at the shutter. Drawn in code over the painting: RUGCO EMPIRE in neon on the garage's board (its P won't stay lit, and the light runs down the shutter), RUGS in blue neon slanted to its sign's frame, the carpet band at the top of the tower (its zig-zags creep along as if it's still being woven, a fringe at each end), souls rising into the eye, and the eye itself: half asleep on Baby Bull, watching him on Regular Bull, red, locked on and trembling on Raging Bull, red and wide open on Impossibull, with a the tower's windows blacking out and lighting back up into it, the pavilion blazing, a swell, a glance at the panel and a reaction of its own when the mode changes. The logo is UNRUGGABULL II in the block face with a dark outline, a short burnt edge and a glint on the II (`A.logo()`), with SALVATION FOR THE UNRUGGED on a dark band under it. Under the picture an elevator panel picks the difficulty: a lit call button per mode with a Doom-style bull face that looks around, blinks and reacts when picked (Raging snorts steam, Impossibull fire), the picked button's ring on fire (a candle's on Baby up to crimson on Impossibull), a readout with the mode's name and line (EXTRA HEALTH., REGULAR HEALTH., NO HEALTH RESETS., NO HEALING.), Start, a sound button, a full screen button (where the browser has full screen) and Exit (back to JonniePeed Games), and CREDITS. Upright the painting fills the screen above the panel, cropping sky first on short phones; only when its safe zone (the logo's sky down to his feet) won't fit at full width does it shrink, with dark sides (a short 16:9 phone, a tablet). - **Controls.** A thumb stick on the left and Shoot and Slash on the right. The touch controls follow the last input: they show on a touch screen or after a touch, and moving the mouse or pressing a key hides them until the next touch (a touch-screen laptop; owner, Oct 14). Hold Shoot to keep firing (five shots a second) or tap for one; shots lean toward the nearest temp or carpshit in a cone ahead. Like the first game, the blaster runs on charges: 20, shown under the hearts, with one back every half second. Run dry and it overheats: an "OVERHEAT" pop, a power-down with a vent of steam, and for a second (`TUNE.overheat`) nothing recharges; then it recharges as usual but won't fire until it has cooled back to 3 charges (`TUNE.cool`). Pull the trigger meanwhile and it only sputters (a choked zap and a few electric crackles, ragged sparks and a puff of smoke at the muzzle). So holding Shoot on an empty gun gets short bursts with pauses (about 1.2 shots a second, against 2 for the old trickle); letting go refills it. A chirp says it's full again. It works this way on every floor (`overheat`, `sputter` and `hot` in `game.js`; the 48th Floor's `topdown.js` uses the same calls). The 48th Floor, where aiming is firing, has its own numbers: 0.2 seconds, then 12 charges. Keys: A/D or arrows to move, W, Up or Space to jump, S, C or Down to crouch, Shift or ' to shoot (J or Z too), Enter to slash (K or X too). On every floor the mouse's left button fires (held, it keeps firing) and its right button slashes. Keys are read by position, so holding Shift doesn't change them. M sound, F full screen, P or Escape pause. On the title card Enter or Space starts, as on every other card. **The blaster only where there's a fight** (owner, Oct 15): both elevator lobbies and the quiet ride are katana only (`floor.blasterOff()`): Shoot, its keys and the left mouse button give a dry click (`sputter`), the charge bar hides, the Shoot pad greys out with no ring; Slash works as everywhere. The long ride keeps the blaster for the whole ride. **No Slash before the katana:** in the stairwell's first section the Slash button is hidden but keeps its place, so Shoot never moves; it pops in when he gets the katana (`floor.katana()`), and a continue before the katana hides it again. Enter, K, X and the right mouse button do nothing there, as before. On touch screens, Start goes full screen and the touch controls appear (where, under **Screen** below). The stick has no frame, just a faint ring where it rests, and it floats: put your thumb down anywhere in its area and that's the centre (a hint under it shows until the first touch). Slide left or right to move, flick up to jump (once per flick; come back toward the middle to flick again), hold down to crouch. If the thumb wanders well past the ring, the centre follows it. Shoot and Slash are **Ghost** buttons ([cards-plan.md](cards-plan.md)): dark glass, a thin cream ring and the pixel blaster or katana, no labels (their names are for screen readers); held, the glass lights up and the button sinks a little. A thin ring round Shoot is the blaster's charge (sky blue, red when low; none, the icon dimmed and a line across, while the blaster is down or put away); Slash's gold ring sweeps back as it's ready again. The sticks are a faint ring and a soft knob, gold while held (the 48th's aim stick blue), and Pause is the same dark glass. In portrait Shoot sits over Slash, and the title card says it's best played in landscape; in landscape Slash sits a little higher than Shoot. A finger can slide between Shoot and Slash, and a touch in the gap between them counts for the nearer one.

In play (playing, paused and the rugged and clear cards) the game fills the window on every device, in or out of full screen; full screen only hides the browser's bars. The title screen and the intro fill the window too, the title with its own layout (see **Title and story**). The page stays blank until the title draws (`body.booting`). Start goes full screen on a touch screen, not on a computer. `layoutPlay()` in game.js places everything. On a wide screen the picture is the biggest 16:9 picture that leaves room for Pause, a side band of 56 px or a 48 px band on top, whichever is bigger (640 × 360 on a Galaxy S25 sideways, 1280 × 720 on a 16:10 monitor), and Pause is an icon at the top right of that band, clear of the HUD; the stick, Shoot and Slash keep their landscape places, overlapping the picture's corners a little. Upright, the picture runs edge to edge with its bottom on the halfway line, the controls start 20 px below it at the same height on every stage, Pause sits above the picture's right corner, and the tower dial above the picture shows where he is in RugCo Tower: a notch a floor from the ground to the roof, the floor he's on marked with its number, and the department's name (`dialAt`: the stairwell's marker climbs with him, the rides follow the elevator's indicator), hidden in cutscenes.   - **Raging Bull:** no box and no quiet-ride crate; the long ride's crate is a trick (below). Coffee and the katana still heal.

## Roof rules

The lead-in and the crescendo follow the approved [roof plan](roof-plan.md) and [storyboard](roof-storyboard.png); the fight between them follows [roof-rework-plan.md](roof-rework-plan.md) (MAXIMIZE CARPETS, agreed with the owner on his phone, Oct 14). Motion, light, audio, the music system and the art (Part B, `roof-art.js`) are built. `unruggabull-ii/#stage=roof` starts it directly; combine it with `&seed=42` to replay. Roof bests use a separate local key.

- **The roof is a grid:** 5 tiles across the aisle and 3 deep in front of him (`ROOF.grid`; rows .15 of the hall from z −0.05). He moves a tile a step on the stick or the arrows/WASD (up and down change rows), repeating every 0.14 seconds while held after a 0.2-second first step, and slides to the tile's middle. The roof has no crouch and no jump (up and down are rows; Space does nothing here), so it uses the same pads as everywhere: the stick, Shoot and Slash. The pause card's Controls list says so (`controlsRows()`: up the roof and back, a tile at a time and then freely once the souls come back, a finger or held mouse on the picture, the souls firing on their own). Every attack is dodged by stepping. Charges refill every 0.35 seconds here.
- **Lead-in:** from the long ride, the roof takes over as he turns in the car's doorway, in one shot (`ROOF.handoff`, 1.6 seconds): the camera follows him out through the doorway. ## The ending

After dawn, the ending plays as the roof's `ending` state, then the roof's clear card. It follows [ending-plan.md](ending-plan.md) (storyboarded with the owner, Oct 14), drawn with the roof's art (`roof-art.js`, handed to it as `art`), with its timing, music, skip and results settled with him on Oct 17 (the plan's **Settled with him**). It's out of his hands: no moving or shooting (`R.phase` is `scene`, so the low-health heartbeat stops too), the touch controls go away (the floor's `scene()`), and the HUD fades out over the first shot and stays away behind the clear card (`hudAlpha()`). Pause still works. The run's time stops where the ending starts (`Q.endingFrom`), so the clear card's time is what it was before there was an ending; souls and the displayed count don't change. It counts as stage 15 (cleared) in the reports.

Six shots (`SHOTS` in `ending.js`; times in `E`):
- **Dawn breathes** (2.5 s, on the roof): the roof's dawn held a moment longer while the HUD fades.
- **The tower at sunrise** (6 s, a picture): the owner's picture of RugCo Tower at a dusty sunrise (`ending-stills.js`), panned up from its broken roof to the sky over the shot while souls rise out of the roof; p(Loom) stands wrecked on the glass pavilion (his ChatGPT edit, Oct 16), with code touches over it (`loomTouches`: smoke curling off it, loose threads swaying, embers, the sunrise sliding once across its dead eye), the reverse of the intro's still 5.
- **The dead eye** (6.9 s, drawn in code): p(Loom)'s wrecked eye, close, in its riveted socket on the shuttle under slack threads, the rug along the bottom, in the dawn light (the roof's close-up, `closeUp`). At 3.1 s (dark a beat and a half longer, owner, Oct 16) a tiny blue light (Loomy's) fades up in the pupil; at 4 s its iron lids blink once with the clack from tapping Loomy in the intro (`armorClank`); at 4.8 s the iris floods red, the cracks glow and spark, the frame shakes (not with reduced motion), with `zap` and `rumble`.
- **It tears loose** (4 s, on the roof): the eye is red and lights the roof; he raises the blaster; it jerks on its rail, the shuttle with it; four cables snap (`cableSnap` at 0.6, 0.8, 1 and 1.2 s); at 1.3 s it rips out with a white flash, `snap`, `machineBreak` and a shake, and flies straight up out of the picture by 2.1 s (`swoop`), trailing a thread. - **Final card** (about 5 s): the reaction fades to black in six steps over 0.8 s; "... TO BE CONTINUED..." types out with the narrator's blips, a beat of 0.55 s, then "MAYBE.", in the game's lettering at one and a half times its usual size.

**Music** (owner, Oct 17, "like a prog rock album"): one piece from the snap to the dead eye, the `ending` track in `music-roof.js` (written by `tools/unruggabull-ii/roof-music.js`), started by `roofCue('dawn')` as the dawn opens; the snap itself still hard-stops the fight's music and plays the chord hook (`chord`). ## The stairwell rules

The prologue, designed in [stairwell-plan.md](stairwell-plan.md), on the arena's side-view engine with a ground line for the stairs. ## The 48th Floor rules

Built from [floor48-plan.md](floor48-plan.md). p(Loom)'s soul-reclamation factory, seen from above at a slight tilt, like Smash TV: the third engine (`topdown.js`). The quiet ride from the 13th Floor opens its doors on the factory's glow and he walks out into it.

- **Controls.** Twin sticks. Keys: WASD move; the mouse aims (a small reticle), its left button fires and its right button slashes; the arrow keys aim and fire; Space, Enter, K or X slash; Shift, J, Z or ' fire where he's moving or last aimed (Shift fires here as on every other floor; owner, Oct 15). On touch screens the move stick stays on the left and an aim stick takes the place of Shoot and Slash (no Slash button: decided by the owner, Oct 14): it floats like the move stick, fires while held past a small dead zone, and a flick (out and released within 0.22 seconds) slashes the way it went out (its furthest point, so a thumb that comes back toward the middle before lifting still slashes the right way); a tap (released within 0.22 seconds, under 22 pixels) slashes at the nearest threat, bobbins first. In landscape the two sticks sit in the bottom corners (owner, Oct 14: the aim stick had sat mid-screen), in areas of the same size so their resting rings mirror each other; upright they sit at one height, each 88 px in from its edge. The move stick is analog. The blaster leans up to 0.2 radians toward the nearest carpshit in its line on the stick (0.16 on keys, 0.07 on the mouse), and toward a machine only when no carpshit is in the line. If he isn't aiming, the slash goes at the nearest threat in reach, a bobbin or thread on its way first. A slash catches a bobbin or thread within 60 pixels of him from any side (`TD.parry`), not only in its arc (owner, Oct 13: deflects should be easier).
- **Unruggabull** is drawn at half size from his existing poses (`half()` keeps the outline on edges): from behind when aiming up, the side poses aiming left or right, the front view aiming down, so he always faces his aim. 108 pixels a second; five hearts, 1.5 seconds of safety after a hit, and a knock back.

Run dry, it overheats as on every floor, with milder numbers: 0.2 seconds with no recharge, then it fires again at 12 charges. **The katana:** a 1.35-radian arc 46 pixels deep where he aims with a 7.5-pixel lunge that way (a quarter of the first build's: the owner found it disorienting), 0.32 seconds apart. It kills any carpshit outright (armor and rolls included; a shag takes 5 and staggers), knocks bobbins back at whoever threw them (3 charges back), cuts threads out of the air and cuts cables, and does 4 damage to a machine; a kill with it gives 2 charges back. ## Writing and auditioning music

`music-roof.js`, `music-floor.js` and `music-elevator.js` load before `audio.js`. Each track is one plain object with `bpm`, `key`, `swing` (0–0.45), a named `patterns` map, an `order` list, a zero-based `loop` index into that list, and initial `stems`. Each pattern declares its length in bars (16 steps each) and named channels. Here is a one-bar example:

```js
{
  bpm: 160, key: 'Dm', swing: 0, loop: 0, order: ['A'], stems: ['lead'],
  patterns: { A: { bars: 1, channels: {
    pulse1: { stem: 'lead', wave: 'pulse', duty: .25, vol: .07,
      notes: 'D5 - F5 - A5 - D6 - . . . . . . . .' }
  } } }
}
```

A note name starts a note; `-` holds it and `.` rests for a step. An array can also contain objects such as `{note:'D5', len:2, chord:['F5','A5'], vol:.06, envelope:{attack:.01,decay:.04,sustain:.5,release:.08}, vibrato:{rate:6,depth:.01}, arp:[0,7,12], slide:'A4', echo:{delay:.2,feedback:.25,repeats:2}}`. Length is in steps; envelope, slide duration and echo delay are seconds; arp intervals are semitones (`arpHz` sets their rate); vibrato depth is a fraction of frequency. Channel defaults accept these effects too. Use `wave:'pulse'` with duty `.125`, `.25` or `.5` for either lead, `triangle` for bass, `noise` for drums (`k/s/h`, plus heavy `K/S`, open hat `H`, crash `c`, tom `T`), and `sawtooth` or `square` for a heavy lead. Noise presets combine filtered noise and pitched drops, with a channel/note envelope. Detuned pulse chords supply C's placeholder choir. Echo repeats are capped at four.

A channel with `drive: true` plays through the overdrive (Saga II's guitars, the shuffle's guitar). The 13th Floor's shuffle is written in `tools/unruggabull-ii/floor-music.js` (chord lists, lead lines and drum bars expanded by small helpers): edit it there and run `node tools/unruggabull-ii/floor-music.js`, which rewrites `music-floor.js`. Saga II itself is written as short strings in `tools/unruggabull-ii/roof-music.js` (power-chord riffs where `x` is a palm-muted chug and `O` an open chord, lead lines, drum bars and choir chords) and expanded into this format: edit it there and run `node tools/unruggabull-ii/roof-music.js`, which rewrites only `roofA`, `roofAHeavy` and `ending` (the ending's music, built from the heavy track's SOLO, so a change to the solo carries into it).

Add an object under a new ID in `UnrugRoofMusic`, or call `UnrugSound.registerTrack(id, data)` to audition edited data. An edit to the playing ID replaces it on the next bar, so notes are not cut mid-bar. Update the candidate IDs in the cue selection if adding a fourth direction. `stems([...])` and pattern-track `tempo(rate)` apply at the next scheduled bar, leaving active notes and echoes intact. `music(id,{boundary:'beat'|'bar'})` queues a switch; `music(id,{slam:true})` starts a fresh downbeat immediately. For tracks with `startPattern`/`holdPattern`, `music(id,{pattern:'heavy',slam:true})` jumps to a named section, which repeats until another game cue. `loopPattern:true` can opt any selected pattern into that hold. Horns Up starts on its `muzak`, held; when p(Loom) takes the cable the music hard-cuts, and the reel selects `reel`, which plays once and leads into `riff`, `breakdown` and `solo`, looping from `riff`. `brownout(seconds)` muffles and sags whatever's playing until it stops. Live replacement preserves a held section. `music(null,{hard:true})` cuts the transport and music overlays; `stinger('spool')` plays a phrase over the music. The original section format and its synthesis remain unchanged. A pattern channel with `synth: 'classic'` plays with that synthesis instead (the section tracks' tones and drum letters, `k s h o r c T K S`): Horns Up is the original storyboard's section track (in `tools/unruggabull-ii/long-ride/synth.js`) compiled note for note into patterns by `node tools/unruggabull-ii/horns-up.js`, which rewrites only `hornsUp`.
