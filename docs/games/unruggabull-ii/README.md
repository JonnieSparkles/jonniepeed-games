# Unruggabull II

Built from [spec.md](spec.md); why things are the way they are, and what was tried and dropped, is in [history.md](history.md). The game is at `site/unruggabull-ii/index.html`. This build joins the whole climb in one run: the 13th Floor, the plain ride, the 48th Floor and its lobby, the long ride and the roof. Start plays the 13th Floor; its clear has **Take the elevator** as the main button and **Play again** as the secondary one. `#stage=floor48&seed=42` plays the 48th Floor directly (`&at=lobby` starts in its lobby); `#stage=elevator&seed=42` plays the long ride directly; `#stage=roof&seed=42` plays the roof directly. The ending after dawn is still planned. It's unlisted: no card on either shelf, only its direct link, and it keeps `<meta name="robots" content="noindex">` and has local best scores only. The original game, Unruggabull: RugCo Alley, stays in its own repo.

## Finding and promoting the demo

Go to `unruggabull-ii/` directly; nothing on the studio page links to it. To list it, add a card to `site/index.html` (on Side B, a `data-side="b" data-badge="demo" hidden` card like Stick Army's; the thumbnail is `unruggabull-ii/thumb.webp`) and update the studio harness's card counts. To promote it after approval, give it a Side A card, remove the noindex tag, update this doc and the README, run the checks below, stamp last and publish by hand.

## the 13th Floor rules

- **Title and story.** The title screen is RugCo Tower at sunset, souls drifting up to p(Loom)'s beacon. The first Start on a device tells the story so far over it, typed out in narrator blips: a tap shows the whole line, another moves on, Skip or Escape ends it. After that, Start goes straight to the floor and the title card has a **Watch the story** link.
- **Controls.** A thumb stick on the left and Shoot and Slash on the right. Hold Shoot to keep firing (five shots a second) or tap for one; shots lean toward the nearest temp or carpshit in a cone ahead. Like the first game, the blaster runs on charges: 20, shown under the hearts, with one back every half second. Run dry and it powers down with an "EMPTY" pop, clicks and flashes red when you try, and only fires as charges return; a chirp says it's full again. Keys: A/D or arrows to move, W, Up or Space to jump, S, C or Down to crouch, Shift or ' to shoot (J or Z too), Enter to slash (K or X too). On every floor the mouse's left button fires (held, it keeps firing) and its right button slashes. Keys are read by position, so holding Shift doesn't change them. M sound, F full screen, P or Escape pause. On touch screens, Start goes full screen and the controls appear under the scene (in landscape, in the bottom corners). The stick has no frame, just a faint ring where it rests, and it floats: put your thumb down anywhere in its area and that's the centre (a hint under it shows until the first touch). Slide left or right to move, flick up to jump (once per flick; come back toward the middle to flick again), hold down to crouch. If the thumb wanders well past the ring, the centre follows it. Shoot and Slash are round arcade buttons, blue with a pixel blaster and red with a pixel katana, that sink and glow while held. The ring round Shoot is the blaster's charge (red when low, grey while the blaster is down); the ring round Slash sweeps back as it's ready again. In portrait the scene sits right on top of the stick's area and Shoot over Slash, and the title card says it's best played in landscape; in landscape Slash sits a little higher than Shoot. A finger can slide between Shoot and Slash, and a touch in the gap between them counts for the nearer one.
- **Crouch.** Held on the ground only: half height, so anything at head height passes over, but no moving or shooting (slashing still works); on touch screens Shoot greys out while you're down. Jumping from a crouch works. Standing on a pulling runner, crouching grips it: it drags you at a quarter of the speed ("GRIP!"). Carried along crouched (under moving day's planks, or gripping), he shuffles low on two poses of his own (`crouchWalkA`/`B`).
- **Slash** cuts carpshits in two, knocks paper wads, bundles and staples back where they came from (a deflect, which also gives the blaster 3 charges back and flashes the charge bar), and on a pulling runner cuts the rug. Shots go straight through paper, so slashing it back or dodging are the only answers. Temps are safe from the katana behind their desks: the blaster or their own paper gets them.
- **Unruggabull talks**: about seventeen short lines in a small caption at the top left, under the hearts, each once per run (the floor's start, the first deflect, moving day ("LIFT WITH YOUR LEGS."), lights out, the first Spread Shot and coffee, a streak, the copy room, an answer to the Shredder's opening line, the first jam and smash, one heart left, the clear, and "WHOA!" when he's rugged, "HEY! THAT WAS MINE." when the surge takes his blaster, and "CLEAN SWEEP." for his first formation wipe). Long lines wrap into short ones, so the caption stays at the left, clear of the signs hanging over the hall. He talks in his own blips for now; the lines in `LINES` in `game.js` are the recording script for Jonnie's voice. He never talks over the Shredder.
- **Unruggabull** swings his arms as he runs (with a little bob), throws them up to jump, raises the blaster beside his head to shoot (bolts and the muzzle flash come from there, with a kick), swings the katana from over his shoulder, and flinches when hit. He jumps a fifth of the hall high for 0.65 seconds, stretching on takeoff and squashing on landing, with a shadow that shrinks as he rises; at the top he clears low paper and carpshits. Landing kicks up dust. Hit, he staggers a step back toward you, rocking side to side (drawn only). Standing about, he breathes; after 2 seconds he glances left and right; after 5 he does something, in turn: spins the blaster round his finger, checks the katana in its sheath, scuffs a hoof. Moving, shooting, slashing, jumping or crouching resets it. At the clear he throws the horns up.
- **Incoming paper** has a jagged edge that blinks red and orange (never a round glow, so it can't pass for a pickup) and is drawn a little bigger than paper you knocked back. The Shredder's bundles are crumpled balls of ruled paper (blue rules, a red margin) that tumble and shed strips behind them; its staples come four to a throwing star, points out, spinning as they fly. Nothing marks the floor. Flying carpshits ripple as they fly.
- **Carpshits at work** line the walls from far down the hall, about one every 6 to 8 seconds, each section with its own mix (`work` in `BEATS`): Accounts Payable and All Staff have them typing (half of those in an accountant's green visor), on a call in a headset with a chart of Q4 rug targets going down behind them, asleep at the desk (Zs, drool, the monitor on its screensaver), two gossiping at the water cooler, and a manager pacing on the phone in an office set into the wall (a real room behind a window, seen at a changing angle as you pass). The Copy Room has them copying themselves (lying on the copier's glass while copies pile up), typing and asleep. They never fight back. When nothing that's after him is ahead, the blaster leans out to them (`aimIdle`, a wider cone than for temps, in the hall only, never in the fight), and their paperwork flies when they're hit; and a shot frees the soul inside (one per carpshit; the cooler has two), counting double in the dark, where their eyes glow; a shot through the office's glass cracks it. Stations leave them room: none on that side right where one sits.
- **Pickups** float at jump height, so you jump to grab them, and are made to stand out: a beam of light from the ceiling, a glow on the floor, a halo, an outline that flashes white, sparkles going round, and a twinkle when one turns up. A RugCo coffee gives a heart back (never past five). A Spread Shot fills the blaster and fires three bolts at a time for 10 seconds. A coffee and a Spread Shot come after moving day, a coffee after lights out; temps drop a coffee one time in five when you're hurt; the Shredder drifts one in when the fight starts and at each phase change, if you're hurt.
- **Rows** span the whole aisle, so the only way past is a jump: rolling office chairs packed side by side in All Staff, with caution tape along their backs so there's plainly no way through (high backs, five-star bases, spinning casters and a swivel wobble; they tip over if one hits you), every 7 to 10 seconds. (The Copy Room's paper jam sheets are gone: they cut through the desks and didn't read as paper.) While a row or a box is 0.06 to 0.45 seconds away, the floor under it pulses yellow: that's the moment to jump. There's a little cushion: a jump clears a row from 0.04 of the hall's height and a box from 0.045, so the tail of a jump still makes it. The first section has none, so jumping isn't needed straight away. The first row shows a "JUMP" prompt and the first pickup "JUMP TO GRAB".
- **Words on screen** are kept few. Prompts during play are one short line in a small strip at the top, never across the hall: JUMP, DUCK, JUMP TO GRAB, STEP OFF OR SLASH, SLASH THE RUG, RIDE THE RUG, SLASH IT BACK, WIN A RALLY FOR IT, each once. A deflect pops just its charges ("+3"); kills in the dark don't pop "×2" (the meter shows it).
- **Feel.** Only one "EMPTY" shows at a time, and a pop with the same words close by isn't repeated. Kills pause the action for a few frames, blaster hits throw sparks, and souls freed in a quick streak ring a step higher each time. Each freed soul floats up, then flies into the souls counter, which pulses as it lands (the count shown catches up as they land). At one heart the screen edges pulse red with a heartbeat. Now and then a desk phone rings down the hall, and a copier whirs in the copy room. A streak of 10 in the hall drops a Spread Shot just ahead of you ("10 STREAK!"), once per section or event. **Play stats** are wired up but switched off (`"reporting": false` in `stats/games.json`) until Jonnie turns them on. A run starts at Start (a continue at the Shredder is a run of its own), ends at the clear or rugged card, and a restart from pause reports as a quit. Best souls and fastest clear are kept in `localStorage` (`unruggabull-ii-best`), whether the story has been seen in `unruggabull-ii-story`, and sound mute in `unruggabull-ii-muted`.

## Roof rules (Part A)

The approved [roof plan](roof-plan.md) and [storyboard](roof-storyboard.png) are implemented from elevator to dawn. Motion, light, audio and the music system are built; final art, music composition and phone tuning remain in Part B; current drawing uses shapes, existing sprites and the game font. `unruggabull-ii/#stage=roof` starts it directly; combine it with `&seed=42` to replay. Normal Start still plays the 13th Floor, whose clear does not advance to the roof. Roof bests use a separate local key.

- **Lead-in:** doors open over four seconds, then control and eight seconds of slow walking past units and a tank. One white frame reveals the loom, with half a second of raised ambient light. Its eye opens, rides to the middle, and the P(LOOM) card appears. Its orange readout types “HELLO, UNRUGGABULL.”, “I HAVE MODELED THIS MEETING.” and “PROBABILITY OF YOUR SURVIVAL: 1%.” The bar fills to 99%, then FIGHT and the roof music hook start round 1.
- **Three spools:** each round breaks one spool. The odds stay at 99% throughout, flashing with “NO CHANGE.” at every snap. Volleys arrive every six seconds. Once the next paced opening is due, completing a volley stalls the eye white-hot for 2.3 seconds; only blaster hits during that window hurt its spool (10 hits each). Earlier volleys leave it closed. The eye follows the player between openings. Nominal 30/40/45-second rounds space two openings per round; damage ends the round, so a missed opening extends it rather than adding health.
- **Caught souls:** carpshits fly from the cloth in groups of 2/3/4, every 2.8/2.5/2 seconds. Groups keep their spacing using the existing formation movement; each kill counts a soul and its wisp flies into the warp with CAUGHT. A roof-only run starts with `ROOF.initialCaught = 100` earlier-floor captives, already in the counter and faintly visible during the walk. All captives are drawn in six rows of ten thread gaps, with shallow overlapping layers: about 60 distinct silhouettes at the quiet and a counter in the 200s. Every captive gets its own return flight; only simultaneous flight drawing is capped (96), never counts. Dawn raises the same captured population, without scoring it twice.

Cut-ins hold held keys and buffered actions and freeze all fight timers; they contain no threats.

## The 48th Floor rules

Built from [floor48-plan.md](floor48-plan.md). p(Loom)'s soul-reclamation factory, seen from above at a slight tilt, like Smash TV: the third engine (`topdown.js`). The plain ride from the 13th Floor opens its doors on the factory's glow and he walks out into it.

- **Controls.** Twin sticks. Keys: WASD move; the mouse aims (a small reticle), its left button fires and its right button slashes; the arrow keys aim and fire; Space, Shift, Enter, K or X slash; J, Z or ' fire where he's moving or last aimed; E uses a supply. On touch screens the move stick stays on the left and an aim stick takes the place of Shoot and Slash (the Slash button is hidden for a test, owner Oct 13): it floats like the move stick, fires while held past a small dead zone, and a flick (out and released within 0.22 seconds) slashes the way it went out (its furthest point, so a thumb that comes back toward the middle before lifting still slashes the right way); a tap (released within 0.22 seconds, under 22 pixels) slashes at the nearest threat, bobbins first. The move stick is analog. The blaster leans up to 0.2 radians toward the nearest carpshit in its line on the stick (0.16 on keys, 0.07 on the mouse), and toward a machine only when no carpshit is in the line. If he isn't aiming, the slash goes at the nearest threat in reach, a bobbin or thread on its way first. A slash catches a bobbin or thread within 60 pixels of him from any side (`TD.parry`), not only in its arc (owner, Oct 13: deflects should be easier).
- **Unruggabull** is drawn at half size from his existing poses (`half()` keeps the outline on edges): from behind when aiming up, the side poses aiming left or right, the front view aiming down, so he always faces his aim. 108 pixels a second; five hearts, 1.5 seconds of safety after a hit, and a knock back.
- **The blaster:** eight shots a second, 24 charges, one back every 0.18 seconds, one damage a bolt. **The katana:** a 1.35-radian arc 46 pixels deep where he aims with a 7.5-pixel lunge that way (a quarter of the first build's: the owner found it disorienting), 0.32 seconds apart. It kills any carpshit outright (armor and rolls included; a shag takes 5 and staggers), knocks bobbins back at whoever threw them (3 charges back), cuts threads out of the air and cuts cables, and does 4 damage to a machine; a kill with it gives 2 charges back. ## Writing and auditioning music

`music-roof.js`, `music-floor.js` and `music-elevator.js` load before `audio.js`. Each track is one plain object with `bpm`, `key`, `swing` (0–0.45), a named `patterns` map, an `order` list, a zero-based `loop` index into that list, and initial `stems`. Each pattern declares its length in bars (16 steps each) and named channels. Here is a one-bar example:

```js
{
  bpm: 160, key: 'Dm', swing: 0, loop: 0, order: ['A'], stems: ['lead'],
  patterns: { A: { bars: 1, channels: {
    pulse1: { stem: 'lead', wave: 'pulse', duty: .25, vol: .07,
      notes: 'D5 - F5 - A5 - D6 - . . . . . . . .' }
  } } }
}
```

A note name starts a note; `-` holds it and `.` rests for a step. An array can also contain objects such as `{note:'D5', len:2, chord:['F5','A5'], vol:.06, envelope:{attack:.01,decay:.04,sustain:.5,release:.08}, vibrato:{rate:6,depth:.01}, arp:[0,7,12], slide:'A4', echo:{delay:.2,feedback:.25,repeats:2}}`. Length is in steps; envelope, slide duration and echo delay are seconds; arp intervals are semitones (`arpHz` sets their rate); vibrato depth is a fraction of frequency. Channel defaults accept these effects too. Use `wave:'pulse'` with duty `.125`, `.25` or `.5` for either lead, `triangle` for bass, `noise` for drums (`k/s/h`, plus heavy `K/S`, open hat `H`, crash `c`, tom `T`), and `sawtooth` or `square` for a heavy lead. Noise presets combine filtered noise and pitched drops, with a channel/note envelope. Detuned pulse chords supply C's placeholder choir. Echo repeats are capped at four.

A channel with `drive: true` plays through the overdrive (Saga II's guitars, the shuffle's guitar). The 13th Floor's shuffle is written in `tools/unruggabull-ii/floor-music.js` (chord lists, lead lines and drum bars expanded by small helpers): edit it there and run `node tools/unruggabull-ii/floor-music.js`, which rewrites `music-floor.js`. Saga II itself is written as short strings in `tools/unruggabull-ii/roof-music.js` (power-chord riffs where `x` is a palm-muted chug and `O` an open chord, lead lines, drum bars and choir chords) and expanded into this format: edit it there and run `node tools/unruggabull-ii/roof-music.js`, which rewrites only `roofA` and `roofAHeavy`.

Add an object under a new ID in `UnrugRoofMusic`, or call `UnrugSound.registerTrack(id, data)` to audition edited data. An edit to the playing ID replaces it on the next bar, so notes are not cut mid-bar. Update the candidate IDs in the cue selection if adding a fourth direction. `stems([...])` and pattern-track `tempo(rate)` apply at the next scheduled bar, leaving active notes and echoes intact. `music(id,{boundary:'beat'|'bar'})` queues a switch; `music(id,{slam:true})` starts a fresh downbeat immediately. For tracks with `startPattern`/`holdPattern`, `music(id,{pattern:'heavy',slam:true})` jumps to a named section, which repeats until another game cue. `loopPattern:true` can opt any selected pattern into that hold.
