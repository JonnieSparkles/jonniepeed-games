# Unruggabull II: history

Why things are the way they are, and what was tried and dropped, so a later change doesn't undo a fix or retry a dead end. Each line names the round it comes from, by its commit title in [the first pull request](https://github.com/JonnieSparkles/jonniepeed-games/pull/35), which tells each round's full story with the bots' numbers. The game as it is now is in [README.md](README.md).

## Why it's this way

- **The crawl became six illustrated stills** (the intro, Oct 13): the owner wanted the story shown, not told: the Rugfather raising p(Loom) like a son, humour in the pictures and a straight face in the narration. The pictures are the owner's, made in ChatGPT; everything moving and every letter is drawn in code, since AI pictures garble text.
- **Each still waits for a tap** (the intro): it moved on 1.8 s after its line at first; the owner wanted people to look and read as long as they like. A blinking arrow (and "Tap" on the first still) shows when.
- **The intro shows the souls becoming carpets** (the intro): "we're still not connecting that p(loom) turned evil". Line 4 says it took the order a little too literally; the shadow outgrows the Rugfather and renames itself p(Loom); souls go into the beacon and carpets unroll down the tower; the ledger says 5 SOULS IN > 1 RUG OUT. The factory itself still waits for the 48th Floor.
- **The baby is Loomy; p(Loom) is the name it gives itself** (the intro): PROJECT DOORMAT was a placeholder. The chalkboard says MAXIMIZE CARPETS, a play on profits.
- **The intro fills the screen and ends in light** (the intro, Oct 13): on the owner's S25 it wasn't full screen in landscape (the header row and padding took a third of the height) and portrait left the picture framed. The header is hidden during the intro; in portrait the caption sits under the picture. The last tap swells the doorway's light over the picture and the floor opens out of it, so the cut into the game isn't bare. Play itself still has the header row in landscape: the HUD fills both top corners, so the buttons have nowhere to float.
- **Start always plays the story, so the stills always load** (stairwell port, Oct 13): the owner, "the title screen start should ALWAYS start at the story imo then u skip from there like every game". The intro had loaded its 290 KB of stills only for a device that hadn't seen it, with a Watch the story link after; both went.
- **The caption is sized to the stage, not the window** (the intro): at the window's size a long line covered a third of the picture and hid the ledger in the game's smaller stage.

- **Roof motion, light and sound preserve the fight** (Part B effects): a separate cosmetic pool lets trapped souls move individually and visibly empty their warp slots without advancing the outcome stream. Flood departure animation compresses into one second while arrivals keep Part A's schedule. Spool hit-stop holds the picture during the existing intermission so bots and player input keep their timing. The lower machine voice retains its caption cadence. Chip-synth tracks and ambience make the crescendo audible; the quiet cuts the sequencer immediately, and the heavy track starts at fusion. Art and phone tuning remain separate.

- **A packed warp and time to fight** (roof Part A follow-up, PR #84): the first framework reached the quiet with only a few captives and cleared in about 2¼ minutes. Earlier-floor captives and more frequent small carp groups now fill the warp and counter; all souls return and rise at dawn. Rows with slight overlap preserve readable silhouettes. Rings moved up the roof with real forward/back movement so their depth matters and phone edges cannot crop them. Spaced openings lengthen the rounds through attacks rather than extra health; roof-only recharge and ring weights keep the larger fight playable without changing the 13th Floor.

- **The roof framework precedes its polish** (roof Part A): the approved storyboard needs a complete playable fight before art, sound and feel can be tuned. Floors share the same core and renderer; the spools gate the three rounds while the 99% odds only yield to the mega stream. Every beat has a replaceable drawing and timing seam, and the 13th Floor's existing seeded outcomes and pictures remain unchanged.

- **Moving day replaced the audit** (notes after the first deploy): "i still dont get the audit thing", and the owner asked for "an athletic furniture section (not too hard) but without having to worry about shooting".
- **Furniture costs a heart** (notes): a version where it only bumped you was tried and dropped the same day: "It's okay if furniture hurts you." The casual bot suffers for it (it reaches lights out in 48% of runs, down from 75%); playtesting wins.
- **The hall is endless until the copy room** (notes): "we shouldnt see shredder right away and instead an endless corridor. shredder slowly starts to get closer as we progress".
- **The surge shows its cause** (notes): "losing your blaster isnt clear why that happens". A blue tell and a lightning bolt to the gun.
- **Shorter slash reach with a longer window and a cut line** (notes): deflects "seem like the bullet i deflected was too far away to hit". Reach 0.24 became 0.19, the window 0.16 s became 0.2 s so timing stays as forgiving, and a line from the blade shows the reach.
- **Few words on screen** (notes): "theres a lot of text on screen thats distracting". Prompts are one short line; deflect and ×2 pops went.
- **Fun over hard** (easier Shredder and rally): the owner's direction for this demo. No phone buzz, no rank, no hard mode (his lines).
- **Rallies are fast, in every phase, with a marathon once a phase** (fast rallies): "boss battle still feels boring … the volley sequences should be way more fast paced and a few longer", after the owner beat the last build easily. Decent bots went from 81% to 54% clears.
- **The slash knocks paper back for 0.16 seconds, not 0.12** (fast rallies): fast returns need a forgiving swing; it made the rallies fairer without slowing them.
- **Formation wipes must be done before halfway, and are a pop, not a big moment, outside lights out** (fast rallies): "formation wipes is a bit much". About 7 a run became about 2.
- **The souls goal is 90** (shredded-paper wads): it was 60 until lights out started paying double, which made 60 a given before the copy room.
- **The power surge can't be dodged** (volley match): "no, the volley should be forced". The blaster flies up the rug, and winning a rally rolls it back; a rolling rug pins you at the back (the owner's pick).
- **Incoming paper has a jagged red edge, no glow and no floor shadow** (lights out, shredded-paper wads): the shadow was confusing, and a glow looked like a power-up.
- **Captions are small, at the top left** (volley match): his lines across the scene got in the way.
- **A thumb stick, with crouch on down** (thumb stick): the owner's idea, as the arrows were tough on a phone. Crouch got jobs straight away: high lines, paper airplanes, gripping the rug.
- **No shooting while crouched** (Shredder's life): the owner leaned yes, but then ducking would be free: you'd duck the paper airplanes and keep shooting, and they'd stop costing anything. Shoot greys out instead, so it's clear.
- **The Shredder reacts instead of getting more attacks** (Shredder's life): "it's there, just needs more life", after the fight had gained plenty of mechanics. It lunges, recoils, laughs, watches you and talks back; its taunts are rationed so they don't nag.
- **The souls' hint is slow, big and holds only while you fire** (polish): the owner didn't catch the first version. Nothing announces it still; the souls are just easier to see, and the streams can't run out while you aren't shooting.
- **The chairs are proper office chairs, and no taller than his jump** (polish): "the chairs u jump over … look terrible". Drawn bigger they stood taller than his jump, which read as unjumpable.
- **The souls' hint is unannounced, white and gold, and never bends in** (mega stream hint): it's the first glimpse of the mega stream, which fuses only on the roof against p(Loom) (the owner's "shining star"). It has to feel like something odd and good happened, not a power-up: Spread Shot already fires three ways in gold, so the streams are the souls' own colours and the souls are seen flying into the gun. Bending in is saved for the middle floor's near miss.
- **No frame round the stick; round arcade buttons** (fast rallies): "the control pad shouldn't have the outer rectangle", and the buttons "can look cooler". Both orientations stay; landscape plays best.
- **A new Unruggabull for the lit hall** (the new bull): the owner wanted a fresh pass for this release that "doesnt need to match the first game completely", "more mature" meaning a step up in quality rather than an older bull. Drawn in code from posable parts, so every frame changes together. He's muscular, with wide shoulders and a narrow waist, after drafts that "looks fat"; he has dark brown fur ("the middle of the dark brown range") and a real neck ("notice how he has more of a neck here"). The face and outfit come from the owner's poster: a big warm-brown pompadour, clear glasses over half-lidded eyes, cream-to-gold horns, an orange-tan snout, the red vest open over a dark tee with grey sleeves, dog tags, rolled cuffs and the earring in his right ear. He keeps the gold blaster ("blaster"), not the poster's pistol.
- **The floor has its own script** (the split): `game.js` had grown to 2,357 lines and the roof needs a floor of its own. The 13th Floor moved to `floor13.js` behind hooks the core calls (update, draw, what the blaster can hit, the HUD), with its update keeping every system in the same order, so seeded runs don't change: 1,200 bot runs replayed identically, and the scenes drew the same pixel for pixel.
- **His hair sweeps back into a ducktail** (the new bull): with lighter fur, the plain back of his head read as a blank face looking at you.
- **On dark brown fur, his hair stands out by being warmer and redder, not lighter** (the new bull): the poster's brown hair works against black fur; on dark brown it would blend in.
- **The hall is lit pixel art at 480 × 270** (the lit renderer): next to the first game, the 13th Floor read as Wolfenstein: flat walls, even light, thin sets and a 26 px bull, where the first game's pull was mood (darkness, fire against neon, a wet floor, a detailed bull). Of three paths the owner picked a renderer that works out every pixel from the hall's shapes, materials and lights, at twice the old size, with every sprite redrawn at 2× in time. `render.js` does it; the hall it draws is worked out from the game's own projection, so everything the game draws sits on the rendered floor and none of the rules moved.
- **30 frames a second** (the lit renderer): on the owner's Galaxy S25 a frame of the lit hall took about 20 ms at 480 × 270, too much for 60 with the game around it. He compared 30 and 60 side by side, blind, and "30 looks fine". The game still steps in 60ths, two to a drawn frame, so it plays the same; the bots, which drive the steps themselves, replay identically.
- **One renderer for the page** (the lit renderer): the first speed test built three renderers in one page, and the browser then compiled the code for some of them half as well, a different one in each browser. Built one at a time, each in a fresh page, the numbers held steady, so the game makes one and keeps it.
- **Glowing things are laid on unlit** (the lit renderer): the threats and rewards have to read at a glance in a dark hall, so bolts, pickups, paper in flight, monitors, switches, the temps' tell and the Shredder's eyes go on as they are, over what's lit, as lights out already did. Everything else takes the light where it stands, and a soft floor of light keeps him, rows and boxes faintly there in the dark.
- **Today's bull for now** (the lit renderer): the new one has 9 of the 27 poses the game uses, and swapping between two bulls mid-run (standing, then the old one running and shooting) looked worse than either. He goes in when the rest of his poses are drawn.
- **No "8-bit" label** (the lit renderer): with the hall lit at 480 × 270 the game isn't 8-bit any more, and the owner chose to drop the label rather than call it 16-bit. The page, the share card's alt text and the spec just call it a sequel. (Unruggabull's voice is still crushed to 8-bit sound.)
- **Moving day keeps the blaster, and its box walls can be blasted** (the lit renderer, the owner's notes): "blaster shouldnt be disabled". With nothing alive to shoot there, three hits break a stack apart, a way through without the gap. The bot still heads for the gap, so its numbers barely move: a temp still up as moving day starts can now be shot, about a soul more a run (over 400 seeds decent clears 199, was 195; casual and expert within a run or two).
- **Moving day says what it wants** (the lit renderer, the owner's notes): "hard to tell what to do". The first wall, beam and row of chairs are marked: arrows on the floor into the gap, DUCK on the beam, JUMP on the chairs.
- **Chairs packed with caution tape** (the lit renderer, the owner's notes): three chairs with gaps between them "looks like u can squeeze through but cant". The rule didn't change; the row now looks like what it is.
- **House lights up early, bolts softer** (the lit renderer, the owner's notes): the lit hall was "a liiiiitttle disorienting with so much flashing", mostly the muzzle flash lighting the hall five times a second. The first sections are a brighter office, the copy room on stays moody, and bolts and the flash light the hall softly.
- **Carpshits at work** (the lit renderer, the owner's idea): "carpshits sitting at desks doing work. something funny like that". Picked from a sheet of seven gags (a coffee queue was dropped). Planned as scenery, but "they do feel like i should be able to shoot them": the owner weighed souls forced to work ("its funnier if its carpshits") and the story settles it, since p(Loom) weaves the souls it catches into carpshits: a shot frees the soul inside. Green visors because the floor is Accounting.
- **The manager's office is a room, not a picture** (the lit renderer): drawn flat on the wall it "looks like a tv screen". It's a room behind a window, running back along the hall behind the wall, with the wall's thickness at the window's edge, so you see into it at a changing angle as you pass.
- **Scenery from far down the hall, the rest fading in** (the lit renderer): things "just pop into view so close to the character"; with the far wall gone, everything appeared where it used to stand. Scenery now starts far down the hall in the fog. What you fight still starts there but fades in out of the dark, because starting it further back changes how long it takes to reach you, which means retuning.
- **Moving day's furniture stays put** (the lit renderer, the owner's notes): its chairs rolled at you like All Staff's, so a row could catch up with the beam ahead and "it looks like u have to crouch and jump at the same time". Left in the hall, nothing rolls, so pieces keep their spacing (beams cost the expert bot 0.14 hearts a run, down from 0.78).
- **Targets only once they're out of the dark** (the lit renderer, the owner's notes): "u can shoot bad guys that are too far off in the distance". Things fading in at the old far end can't be aimed at or hit until they're fully in view (`TUNE.reach`, 0.9 of the way).
- **Hero V, arms relaxed, a dark hilt** (the bull's build): one more pass at his build, over six proportions, picked "relaxed arms hero v": wider shoulders over a narrower waist, a longer torso and thicker arms. His arms had hung "so bowed out", elbows well outside his shoulders; now they hang at his sides. From the front, the katana's gold hilt ran up into his earring and read as "a long gold earring", so the grip is a dark cord wrap with only the guard and cap gold, sitting lower on his shoulder. Bolts still leave from the first bull's muzzle spot (the new one's is within half a unit), so the bots' runs are unchanged.
- **No laser on a deflect, and a quieter opening** (notes): the deflect's line from his blade to the paper read as "a laser beam coming out of you", so a deflect bursts at the paper instead and the swoosh shows the swing. "THE 13TH FLOOR / ACCOUNTING" then "FREE 90 SOULS" sat in a band across the middle for 4.4 seconds, "right in your face" over the reveal of the hall; now each is 1.6 seconds over the ceiling, with no band, fading out, and the subtitle is gone (the section's sign already hangs there). - **Moving day as a course** (notes): moving day was "confusing, and it just doesn't flow right"; the point was "to use some of your athletic skills, not just run around shooting". Box walls to zig-zag round or blast through gave way to a course of jumps and ducks, picked over four playable takes (the course, a rug surf, an office chair ride, a box toss). The owner hated the beam: "you can't tell if you're clearing it or not". Floor tape under the beam read as another low bar, so it went. Souls trapped in bubbles between the pieces give "a little something to make you move left and right". Bumps still cost a heart (the owner's call over a heart-free stumble): the bots got through moving day 97% → 32% (casual), 100% → 90% (decent), 100% → 99% (expert), and cleared 0 / 140 / 387 of 400 (was 0 / 198 / 395).

- **Moving day, simple; a carton, not a pickup; a plank on stepladders** (notes): "the layout of moving day makes no sense. lets just go back to simple for the whole thing. a couple jumps a couple ducks to introduce the mechanics." - **The title and its logo** (the owner's picks): the same scene ("i want the same scene for now"), redrawn at full size: a dithered dusk sky, the city with lit windows and rooftop clutter, RugCo Tower with its window grid and the sunset on its edges, a neon RUGCO sign, finer sun stripes.
