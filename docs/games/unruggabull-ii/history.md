# Unruggabull II: history

Why things are the way they are, and what was tried and dropped, so a later change doesn't undo a fix or retry a dead end. Each line names the round it comes from, by its commit title in [the first pull request](https://github.com/JonnieSparkles/jonniepeed-games/pull/35), which tells each round's full story with the bots' numbers. The game as it is now is in [README.md](README.md).

## Why it's this way

- **The crawl became six illustrated stills** (the intro, Oct 13): the owner wanted the story shown, not told: the Rugfather raising p(Loom) like a son, humour in the pictures and a straight face in the narration. The pictures are the owner's, made in ChatGPT; everything moving and every letter is drawn in code, since AI pictures garble text.
- **Each still waits for a tap** (the intro): it moved on 1.8 s after its line at first; the owner wanted people to look and read as long as they like. A blinking arrow (and "Tap" on the first still) shows when.
- **The intro shows the souls becoming carpets** (the intro): "we're still not connecting that p(loom) turned evil". Line 4 says it took the order a little too literally; the shadow outgrows the Rugfather and renames itself p(Loom); souls go into the beacon and carpets unroll down the tower; the ledger says 5 SOULS IN > 1 RUG OUT. The factory itself still waits for the 48th Floor.
- **The baby is Loomy; p(Loom) is the name it gives itself** (the intro): PROJECT DOORMAT was a placeholder. The chalkboard says MAXIMIZE CARPETS, a play on profits.
- **p(Loom) takes over the narration** (the intro, Oct 14): the owner wanted the story to sound like p(Loom). Its storyboard voice (SAM) can't ship and the game's low 96 Hz stand-in was mumbly on a phone, so it has a `machine` blip voice built from its ingredients. Of the mockups (all SAM, takeovers, an undertone, a machine narrator throughout), he picked a scramble on "It grew up so fast.", with the narrator taking line 6 back for the hero's move: the books are good news for RugCo, so p(Loom) reads them. Line 6's ending became "So Unruggabull had some questions for Accounting. Sharp ones."
- **The intro fills the screen and ends in light** (the intro, Oct 13): on the owner's S25 it wasn't full screen in landscape (the header row and padding took a third of the height) and portrait left the picture framed. The header is hidden during the intro; in portrait the caption sits under the picture. The last tap swells the doorway's light over the picture and the floor opens out of it, so the cut into the game isn't bare. Play itself still has the header row in landscape: the HUD fills both top corners, so the buttons have nowhere to float.
- **Play fills the screen, without the header** (screen space, Oct 15, owner, from the controls audit): on the S25 the header took 48 of 360 px sideways and two rows upright, where another 235 px sat empty above a framed picture, and out of full screen a phone switched to the page layout, which sideways left the controls below the screen. Pause became an icon in a band beside or above the picture, never over it, since the HUD fills the picture's top corners; Sound, Full screen and the way back to the studio moved to the pause card. Sideways he chose full height (640 × 360, 42% more) with the stick, Shoot and Slash where they were, over side bands that keep the picture clear (580 × 326): he didn't want the stick pushed to the far left. Upright the picture runs edge to edge with its bottom on the halfway line, because most people don't hold a phone that low; a HUD band above the picture and a taller, cropped play area were mocked and dropped (the crop hides what comes in from the sides and changes difficulty), and the empty space got the tower dial instead, with only the current floor marked. One layout in or out of full screen, on phones and computers, since a browser can't be kept in full screen; Start doesn't force full screen on a computer, the usual practice for browser games.
- **The controls audit's fixes** (controls, Oct 15, owner, from the audit of every input on every stage): **Shift fires on the 48th Floor** as everywhere else (it had slashed there; Space stays a slash, the factory has no jump). **The blaster only where there's a fight:** the lobbies refused Shoot and supplies but allowed Slash, while the quiet ride fired at nothing; now both lobbies and the quiet ride are katana only, with a dry click so it reads as deliberate, and the long ride keeps the blaster throughout (switching it on when the threads burst in was considered and left out: one rule per stage). **No Slash button before the katana**, rather than a greyed one, so it arrives with the Rugmergency; it keeps its space so Shoot never moves under the thumb. Also: a sound button beside the intro's Skip (the header is hidden there), Enter or Space starts from the title, and the help line's key names (it said J slashes).
- **No carried supplies** (handouts review, Oct 15, owner): Spread Shot, Bull Run and the Spare Vest are gone. With hearts refilled between stages, the quiet ride's crate (the run's only handout) always gave Spread Shot; the Vest's "two hearts or fewer" couldn't happen and Bull Run's turn never came. The Vest was the one that mattered (it lifted the casual bot's first-try clears a lot), and other fixes were weighed (offer it while the run has no continue, or always at the quiet ride with Spread/Bull Run moved to the long ride). He chose instead to drop all three and make the long ride's crate a reward for a ride without a hit (a cap on Regular, a crown on Raging), to come with the difficulty modes. Bots identical on every stage without them: they never used supplies.
- **Start always plays the story, so the stills always load** (stairwell port, Oct 13): the owner, "the title screen start should ALWAYS start at the story imo then u skip from there like every game". The intro had loaded its 290 KB of stills only for a device that hadn't seen it, with a Watch the story link after; both went.
- **The caption is sized to the stage, not the window** (the intro): at the window's size a long line covered a third of the picture and hid the ledger in the game's smaller stage.

- **The roof fight is MAXIMIZE CARPETS** (roof rework, Oct 14): the owner: "roof battle sucks aside from the payoff". What sucked: waiting about 15 seconds for each opening, percentage rings that didn't read, the 99% that never moved, and hitting the eye feeling flat. He wanted something "epic and thematic", an AI trope with carpets and lasers from the eye; he picked the paperclip maximizer (for carpet) and hallucinations, and asked for perpendicular lasers, which became warp and weft. Designed on a playable page on his phone over seven versions ([roof-rework-plan.md](roof-rework-plan.md)). One battle that deteriorates rather than three rounds; every real attack opens the eye, so there's no waiting; the odds fall with every hit and the damage stays on the bar.
- **The reset is a prediction, not a heal** (roof rework): the 99% coming back had to read as p(Loom) being wrong. The bar is stitched over the tear rather than refilled, and the new spools are wound from the caught souls, so its health is literally them; the souls tearing loose for the bells is them quitting p(Loom). His idea, "u think ure defeated ... then u rise up and soul stream"; a knockdown at the reset and a first-hit slam for the beam were offered and left out.
- **Warnings darken and stripe, never red on red** (roof rework, v7): in phase 3 red warnings on red carpet couldn't be told apart. The dark striped fill sits under him in the lit layer, so a warning never hides him either; only the edges glow.
- **Tried and dropped on the design page** (roof rework): the bounce back (shots at the closed eye came back down his column; "annoying"), so shots just clang. Spamming Shoot and Slash together won the first version; a whiff on bare roof rooting him for half a second, and the overheat, keep that from paying. Gusts and the old snares went; the stick's up and down move between rows, so ducking got its own Duck pad and C.
- **Spools shorter after his play** (roof rework, v6): 90 hits a spool on the page took him about 60 seconds a phase ("too long"); the bots land every shot and had shown 40. In the game the blaster fires five a second, so a spool is 40 hits, about 35 seconds a phase for the bots.
- **Open, the eye draws shots from anywhere** (roof port): with the old 0.5 aim cone, shots fired after dodging to an edge flew under the eye, and openings did nothing; the bots showed it before a person would.
- **The beam fires on its own and a finger drags it** (roof rework, the owner): the thumb stick stays for the fight; drag-to-move with auto-fire, dropped for the whole roof earlier, came back for the beam only.
- **A lit roof with no runner, after his first play of the port** (roof rework, Oct 14): "the level looks terrible ... remove the red carpet in the middle. its VERY dark. its hard to see whats going on between all the transparency." The hall's runner was still rendered under the grid, the roof sat at the storm's ambient, and the grid was drawn see-through over both. Now the roof has no runner, the fight's roof is lit (the storm stays in the sky), and every tile is solid: a pale slab or carpet.
- **Carpshits come out of the carpet** (same play): "i thought carp shits spawn from the red tiles?" They did only from carpet further up, and otherwise appeared at the loom, with no moment of hatching. Now a carpet tile heaves for 0.7 seconds and the carpshit peels up out of it, taking the carpet; with no carpet there are none. The furthest row with carpet goes first, so one doesn't hatch in his face (the bots showed it: experts took twice the carpshit hits when any carpet beyond him could hatch).
- **The beam fight as on the finale mockup** (same play): he pointed at the [Mega Stream Finale](https://claude.ai/artifact/MqfeHREtfJp24VcdGqACx4) ("u move the bull and it auto fires to the eye with a 'semi aim' it can veer off if u go to far in one direction. the beam curves and looks awesome") and picked its behind view, busy like it, over its director's cut. So he walks with the beam instead of steering a straight one; its far end trails him (the curve) and leans to the eye; carpshits and heavies pour in for it to melt. First built with the whole tip lagging, the bots spent 90 seconds chasing the racing eye (on it 31% of the time), so only the curve lags and the lean is immediate: the eye goes down in about ten seconds of good tracking, as on the mockup. The pour is a fixed 40, since the beam can stay off the eye for as long as it likes.
- **No Duck on the roof, and the shuttle and the beater instead** (his second play, Oct 14): the high weft read as a laser between two rows with arrows, "its not clear how to avoid". A clearer duck attack was mocked up ([duck lab](https://claude.ai/artifact/CuPsQ62aFoaYpf1EFhR2BP): the beater, a scan plane, the shuttle), and jumping talked through, but on the roof up and down step rows, where everywhere else they jump and crouch, and Duck was a seventh input no other stage has ("thats A LOT of different control schemes"). He kept the grid ("a new mechanic that isnt hard to learn") and Duck went, so the roof has the same pads as everywhere and every attack is dodged by stepping: the shuttle down a column (step aside) and the beater with a gold gap (step into it). FIRST PASS's second pass is now the beater.
- **The shuttle and the beater taken out again** (his third play, Oct 15): "take out the beater and the shuttle ... They look weird and you can't really do anything with them." The fight is back to lasers, needles and carpshits, all dodged by stepping; FIRST PASS has its two passes of columns again.
- **A dead blaster before the souls come back** (same play): in the flood of carpet before the crescendo, "what the hell do I do? We should be able to shoot then and nothing's happening ... a little trick to make the player think ... I can't do anything here." Shots at the closed eye say "NO CHANGE.", and once the music cuts the blaster only clicks and smokes, its bar empty, until the first soul flies into it and it fires gold on its own: the souls are its ammo.
- **Forward and back with the stream** (same play): "moving left and right is great. We should be able to move forward and back too." From the first soul back, up and down move him smoothly up the roof's rows.
- **Carpshits only from further up the roof** (same play): ones hatched from carpet beside him popped up and flew straight past him off the screen. And a tile's white flash when cleared was drawn with the glow, over him, so he looked cut off at a tile; it's drawn under him now.
- **The carpet grabs him** (Oct 15, before his fourth play): standing on red mid-fight, "you take a hit ... you don't really know why". While carpet grips him it now heaves up around his feet, curls its corners up his legs and reaches for him with its fringe, and when it pulls, the rug visibly whips out from under him. Drawing only; the timing is unchanged.
- **An arrival, and p(Loom) rises** (his fourth play, Oct 15): "it shouldnt just pop in", and "theres no real intro leading up to walking on the tiles". He now steps out of the elevator onto a dark roof as the floodlights clunk on a row at a time, and there's no loom until the walk ends: then it rises from behind the far edge, the roof shaking, the lightning catching it on the way up, and its eye flickers before it opens. (He picked rising over powering up in place.)
- **Tile edges under him** (same play: "tiles rendering in front of the bull"): his tile's outline, the grip edge and a warning's edge on his own tile were glow-layer lines across his legs; they're drawn under him now.
- **The beam lasts through the solo, and doesn't stick** (same play): the heavy track's solo, his favourite part, started just as the beam usually finished (about 16 seconds in), so the music cut there. Now the odds hold at 3% until the solo has played and the 1% lands on a bar line, about 24 seconds in. And "the stream shouldnt stick to ploom SO easily. the original mockup felt a little better": the aim assist is gone (the beam's far end just trails him) and the eye glides in a smooth wave, as on the mockup, instead of racing and bouncing.
- **A slash no longer freezes mid-cut** (his fifth play, Oct 15): "if ure mid slash during ... the pre-stream transition, the character freezes and holds that position". The roof skips the bull's update while he isn't in control (the wake, the odds, the fusion, the snap, the dawn); a swing already started now finishes there. Test in case 22.
- **The countdown follows the music; the beam opens the eye at once** (his fifth play, Oct 15): "the pause at 3% works for the solo drop but it doesnt make sense. maybe we just time the defeat draw down better", and before the stream, "its kinda boring ... it should clank but once the stream hits then its vulnerable. right now theres a delay." Offered keeping the eye open through the build-up; I advised against it (the streams would wear the odds down before the beam's moment) and he took clanging until the beam. Now the beam drains from the moment it lands, during p(Loom)'s line; the odds can't fall below a line from 99 to 1% at the solo's end, so on the eye 1% arrives with the solo's last bar and there's no visible hold; and the streams lean to the eye and crack its armour with clangs, the look only. Bots 62 / 98 / 100%.
- **The roof's controls, from the controls audit** (#126's list, Oct 15): Space no longer jumps on the roof (a thumb can't, and there's no jump there); the Controls list says how the roof moves and fires; the dead blaster before the souls come back is `floor.blasterOff()`, so the Shoot pad greys and the charge bar hides as everywhere else the blaster is off. The beam keeps its gold bar: Shoot does nothing there, but the bar shows the unlimited charge.
- **The crescendo as on the mockup** (same play): "I dont know why we have these cut scenes with like the word soul ... cut all of those", three stream lines "stuck there" during the return, and locked in place once the stream starts. The cut-in cards are gone; the blaster fires real bolts on its own, a stream per returned soul, that pinch together; he walks freely from the first soul to the end, including while p(Loom) talks. The reset was ten seconds of "what the hell do I do here" with the roof wall to wall red; he'd liked the design page's version, so a slash flips the carpet back again while p(Loom) re-weaves it.
- **The loom fits the roof, and the eye never goes white on white** (same play): "the loom seems wider than the roof", too close, and "a white circle on white souls on a white loom". The loom was 136 pixels wide where the roof is 70; it's now as wide as the roof, its spools clear of the HUD, the trapped souls at half strength on a dark backing, and the open eye hot yellow with a white core in its dark ring. "Very low resolution": the game is 480 × 270 (his mockups were half that), but the roof's stand-in shapes snapped to whole game pixels; they now draw at half a pixel. Real art is still Part B.

- **Roof motion, light and sound preserve the fight** (Part B effects): a separate cosmetic pool lets trapped souls move individually and visibly empty their warp slots without advancing the outcome stream. Flood departure animation compresses into one second while arrivals keep Part A's schedule. Spool hit-stop holds the picture during the existing intermission so bots and player input keep their timing. The lower machine voice retains its caption cadence. Chip-synth tracks and ambience make the crescendo audible; the quiet cuts the sequencer immediately, and the heavy track starts at fusion. Art and phone tuning remain separate.

- **A packed warp and time to fight** (roof Part A follow-up, PR #84): the first framework reached the quiet with only a few captives and cleared in about 2¼ minutes. Earlier-floor captives and more frequent small carp groups now fill the warp and counter; all souls return and rise at dawn. Rows with slight overlap preserve readable silhouettes. Rings moved up the roof with real forward/back movement so their depth matters and phone edges cannot crop them. Spaced openings lengthen the rounds through attacks rather than extra health; roof-only recharge and ring weights keep the larger fight playable without changing the 13th Floor.

- **The roof framework precedes its polish** (roof Part A): the approved storyboard needs a complete playable fight before art, sound and feel can be tuned. Floors share the same core and renderer; the spools gate the three rounds while the 99% odds only yield to the mega stream. Every beat has a replaceable drawing and timing seam, and the 13th Floor's existing seeded outcomes and pictures remain unchanged.

- **Moving day replaced the audit** (notes after the first deploy): "i still dont get the audit thing", and the owner asked for "an athletic furniture section (not too hard) but without having to worry about shooting".
- **Furniture costs a heart** (notes): a version where it only bumped you was tried and dropped the same day: "It's okay if furniture hurts you." The casual bot suffers for it (it reaches lights out in 48% of runs, down from 75%); playtesting wins.
- **The hall is endless until the copy room** (notes): "we shouldnt see shredder right away and instead an endless corridor. shredder slowly starts to get closer as we progress".
- **The surge shows its cause** (notes): "losing your blaster isnt clear why that happens". A blue tell and a lightning bolt to the gun.
- **Shorter slash reach with a longer window and a cut line** (notes): deflects "seem like the bullet i deflected was too far away to hit". Reach 0.24 became 0.19, the window 0.16 s became 0.2 s so timing stays as forgiving, and a line from the blade shows the reach.
- **Few words on screen** (notes): "theres a lot of text on screen thats distracting". Prompts are one short line; deflect and ×2 pops went.
- **Fun over hard** (easier SHRED-O and rally): the owner's direction for this demo. No phone buzz, no rank, no hard mode (his lines).
- **Rallies are fast, in every phase, with a marathon once a phase** (fast rallies): "boss battle still feels boring … the volley sequences should be way more fast paced and a few longer", after the owner beat the last build easily. Decent bots went from 81% to 54% clears.
- **The slash knocks paper back for 0.16 seconds, not 0.12** (fast rallies): fast returns need a forgiving swing; it made the rallies fairer without slowing them.
- **Formation wipes must be done before halfway, and are a pop, not a big moment, outside lights out** (fast rallies): "formation wipes is a bit much". About 7 a run became about 2.
- **The souls goal is 90** (shredded-paper wads): it was 60 until lights out started paying double, which made 60 a given before the copy room.
- **The power surge can't be dodged** (volley match): "no, the volley should be forced". The blaster flies up the rug, and winning a rally rolls it back; a rolling rug pins you at the back (the owner's pick).
- **Incoming paper has a jagged red edge, no glow and no floor shadow** (lights out, shredded-paper wads): the shadow was confusing, and a glow looked like a power-up.
- **Captions are small, at the top left** (volley match): his lines across the scene got in the way.
- **A thumb stick, with crouch on down** (thumb stick): the owner's idea, as the arrows were tough on a phone. Crouch got jobs straight away: high lines, paper airplanes, gripping the rug.
- **No shooting while crouched** (SHRED-O's life): the owner leaned yes, but then ducking would be free: you'd duck the paper airplanes and keep shooting, and they'd stop costing anything. Shoot greys out instead, so it's clear.
- **SHRED-O reacts instead of getting more attacks** (SHRED-O's life): "it's there, just needs more life", after the fight had gained plenty of mechanics. It lunges, recoils, laughs, watches you and talks back; its taunts are rationed so they don't nag.
- **The souls' hint is slow, big and holds only while you fire** (polish): the owner didn't catch the first version. Nothing announces it still; the souls are just easier to see, and the streams can't run out while you aren't shooting.
- **The chairs are proper office chairs, and no taller than his jump** (polish): "the chairs u jump over … look terrible". Drawn bigger they stood taller than his jump, which read as unjumpable.
- **The souls' hint is unannounced, white and gold, and never bends in** (mega stream hint): it's the first glimpse of the mega stream, which fuses only on the roof against p(Loom) (the owner's "shining star"). It has to feel like something odd and good happened, not a power-up: Spread Shot already fires three ways in gold, so the streams are the souls' own colours and the souls are seen flying into the gun. Bending in is saved for the middle floor's near miss.
- **No frame round the stick; round arcade buttons** (fast rallies): "the control pad shouldn't have the outer rectangle", and the buttons "can look cooler". Both orientations stay; landscape plays best.
- **A new Unruggabull for the lit hall** (the new bull): the owner wanted a fresh pass for this release that "doesnt need to match the first game completely", "more mature" meaning a step up in quality rather than an older bull. Drawn in code from posable parts, so every frame changes together. He's muscular, with wide shoulders and a narrow waist, after drafts that "looks fat"; he has dark brown fur ("the middle of the dark brown range") and a real neck ("notice how he has more of a neck here"). The face and outfit come from the owner's poster: a big warm-brown pompadour, clear glasses over half-lidded eyes, cream-to-gold horns, an orange-tan snout, the red vest open over a dark tee with grey sleeves, dog tags, rolled cuffs and the earring in his right ear. He keeps the gold blaster ("blaster"), not the poster's pistol.
- **The floor has its own script** (the split): `game.js` had grown to 2,357 lines and the roof needs a floor of its own. The 13th Floor moved to `floor13.js` behind hooks the core calls (update, draw, what the blaster can hit, the HUD), with its update keeping every system in the same order, so seeded runs don't change: 1,200 bot runs replayed identically, and the scenes drew the same pixel for pixel.
- **His hair sweeps back into a ducktail** (the new bull): with lighter fur, the plain back of his head read as a blank face looking at you.
- **On dark brown fur, his hair stands out by being warmer and redder, not lighter** (the new bull): the poster's brown hair works against black fur; on dark brown it would blend in.
- **The hall is lit pixel art at 480 × 270** (the lit renderer): next to the first game, the 13th Floor read as Wolfenstein: flat walls, even light, thin sets and a 26 px bull, where the first game's pull was mood (darkness, fire against neon, a wet floor, a detailed bull). Of three paths the owner picked a renderer that works out every pixel from the hall's shapes, materials and lights, at twice the old size, with every sprite redrawn at 2× in time. `render.js` does it; the hall it draws is worked out from the game's own projection, so everything the game draws sits on the rendered floor and none of the rules moved.
- **30 frames a second** (the lit renderer): on the owner's Galaxy S25 a frame of the lit hall took about 20 ms at 480 × 270, too much for 60 with the game around it. He compared 30 and 60 side by side, blind, and "30 looks fine". The game still steps in 60ths, two to a drawn frame, so it plays the same; the bots, which drive the steps themselves, replay identically.
- **One renderer for the page** (the lit renderer): the first speed test built three renderers in one page, and the browser then compiled the code for some of them half as well, a different one in each browser. Built one at a time, each in a fresh page, the numbers held steady, so the game makes one and keeps it.
- **Glowing things are laid on unlit** (the lit renderer): the threats and rewards have to read at a glance in a dark hall, so bolts, pickups, paper in flight, monitors, switches, the temps' tell and SHRED-O's eyes go on as they are, over what's lit, as lights out already did. Everything else takes the light where it stands, and a soft floor of light keeps him, rows and boxes faintly there in the dark.
- **Today's bull for now** (the lit renderer): the new one has 9 of the 27 poses the game uses, and swapping between two bulls mid-run (standing, then the old one running and shooting) looked worse than either. He goes in when the rest of his poses are drawn.
- **No "8-bit" label** (the lit renderer): with the hall lit at 480 × 270 the game isn't 8-bit any more, and the owner chose to drop the label rather than call it 16-bit. The page, the share card's alt text and the spec just call it a sequel. (Unruggabull's voice is still crushed to 8-bit sound.)
- **Moving day keeps the blaster, and its box walls can be blasted** (the lit renderer, the owner's notes): "blaster shouldnt be disabled". With nothing alive to shoot there, three hits break a stack apart, a way through without the gap. The bot still heads for the gap, so its numbers barely move: a temp still up as moving day starts can now be shot, about a soul more a run (over 400 seeds decent clears 199, was 195; casual and expert within a run or two).
- **Moving day says what it wants** (the lit renderer, the owner's notes): "hard to tell what to do". The first wall, beam and row of chairs are marked: arrows on the floor into the gap, DUCK on the beam, JUMP on the chairs.
- **Chairs packed with caution tape** (the lit renderer, the owner's notes): three chairs with gaps between them "looks like u can squeeze through but cant". The rule didn't change; the row now looks like what it is.
- **House lights up early, bolts softer** (the lit renderer, the owner's notes): the lit hall was "a liiiiitttle disorienting with so much flashing", mostly the muzzle flash lighting the hall five times a second. The first sections are a brighter office, the copy room on stays moody, and bolts and the flash light the hall softly.
- **Carpshits at work** (the lit renderer, the owner's idea): "carpshits sitting at desks doing work. something funny like that". Picked from a sheet of seven gags (a coffee queue was dropped). Planned as scenery, but "they do feel like i should be able to shoot them": the owner weighed souls forced to work ("its funnier if its carpshits") and the story settles it, since p(Loom) weaves the souls it catches into carpshits: a shot frees the soul inside. - **The manager's office is a room, not a picture** (the lit renderer): drawn flat on the wall it "looks like a tv screen". It's a room behind a window, running back along the hall behind the wall, with the wall's thickness at the window's edge, so you see into it at a changing angle as you pass.

Scenery now starts far down the hall in the fog. What you fight still starts there but fades in out of the dark, because starting it further back changes how long it takes to reach you, which means retuning.

Left in the hall, nothing rolls, so pieces keep their spacing (beams cost the expert bot 0.14 hearts a run, down from 0.78).
- **Targets only once they're out of the dark** (the lit renderer, the owner's notes): "u can shoot bad guys that are too far off in the distance". Things fading in at the old far end can't be aimed at or hit until they're fully in view (`TUNE.reach`, 0.9 of the way).
- **Hero V, arms relaxed, a dark hilt** (the bull's build): one more pass at his build, over six proportions, picked "relaxed arms hero v": wider shoulders over a narrower waist, a longer torso and thicker arms. His arms had hung "so bowed out", elbows well outside his shoulders; now they hang at his sides.
