# Stick Army

Built from [SPEC-002](../../specs/SPEC-002-stick-army.md), with layout from [SPEC-003](../../specs/SPEC-003-repo-layout.md) and the development shelf from [SPEC-004](../../specs/SPEC-004-side-b.md). The game is at `site/stick-army/index.html`. [SPEC-008](../../specs/SPEC-008-stick-army-campaign.md) is the campaign, 20 waves since the fourth playtest. Stages 1–3 are built: the Dreadnought on wave 20, a victory screen with the squad's roll call, endless play after it, the armored zeppelin, two zeppelins at once, the night raid and the new threats. Stage 4 (endless extras) is planned. It appears only as the **demo** card on Side B; there is no public Side A card. No build step or network service is required. The page keeps `<meta name="robots" content="noindex">` until promotion.

## Rules and input contract

Mouse, keyboard and touch all work. With a mouse, aim and hold the button to fire. On touch, touch and hold to fire, and drag to aim. Arrow keys (or A/D) aim, Space or Enter fires (Enter since round 13), B calls an air strike, C calls fighter cover, P pauses, and F toggles full screen. Each call also has a button beside pause, shown only while the radio holds that call, and the calls held show in the HUD under the score (see Radio calls). The barrel sweeps continuously from slightly below horizontal on the left (`AIM_MIN`) to slightly below horizontal on the right (`AIM_MAX`, `AIM_DIP` = 0.3 rad). The dip is enough to shoot landers at the wall and in the near field and to reach a parked tank. A fully dipped shot skimming the field can also catch a sniper at the edge. When tipped down, the barrel draws in front of the bunker. The page sets `touch-action: manipulation` on everything, so quick repeat taps never zoom on iPhones (pinch zoom still works), while the canvas keeps `none` and the shop keeps `pan-y`. `-webkit-touch-callout: none` on the body stops holding to fire from popping up the iOS callout. The fullscreen button uses the native API where available and fills the window otherwise; on phones under 400 px wide it shrinks to an icon (named for screen readers) to make room for the call buttons. The portrait notebook remains centred in landscape, where (round 15) the round buttons stand in the space beside the page, a column bottom left and full screen bottom right, instead of taking a strip under it, so the page is as tall as the screen allows (on an 844×390 phone, 366 px tall instead of 314). The pause card ("Wave 12 · 14:05 played") and the game-over card show time played (`S.played`, `notePlayed`, `clock`). It counts waves and the shop, not pauses or the title, and never feeds the simulation.

Start with one trampoline and four squad slots. The trampoline is on the left or the right, picked from the run seed (`S.matRight`, `resizeMats`; round 16: "the starting trampoline always starts on the left. That should probably swap sides randomly"); the second trampoline opens across from it and the field hospital's tent stands on the second mat's side, so a right-hand run is the left-hand layout mirrored, drops that avoid the mats included (`pickDropX`). The demo on the title always uses the left. Shoot a body to kill, or pop a chute over the mat to recruit. A falling trooper arriving faster than `CAPTURE_SPEED` (680 logical pixels per second) rips through instead, which in practice only happens to chutes popped right under the planes. There are no guide lines. Larger mats widen the catch area without relaxing the speed limit.

A trooper falling without a chute squashes any enemy he lands on (`crush`), including snipers: 30 points each, with combo. This is the player's skill answer to landers, borrowed from the original Paratrooper.

The turret overheats. Each volley adds `HEAT_PER_SHOT` heat, scaled by the fire-rate upgrade so heat per second stays the same, and heat bleeds off at `COOL_RATE` per second. Reaching full heat locks the gun for `OVERHEAT_LOCK` seconds ("too hot!", steam, a hiss), during which it cools to 35%. A dashed gauge arcs over the dome while the gun is warm, and the barrel reddens as it heats. Each volley also costs `SHOT_COST` points (1, as in Paratrooper), never taking the score below zero. Heat per shot is the same on every wave, so cooling fins (three stacks) always pay off: each cuts heat per shot by 20%.

**Nothing of consequence flies above the page's top rule** (`SKY_TOP`, y 100, where the score, wave and tags are written; round 14, the second win: "nothing 'of consequence' flies above that line. ambient things are no problem"). The high lanes keep a plane's fin and a bomber's tail below it (`SKY_LANES`: planes 120–206, bombers 130–148, zeppelin escorts 120–130; they started at 98, 104 and 98), and so do cargo planes (132–150), heavy bombers (148–168), the air strike (`STRIKE.Y` 130; it was 92, over the score), fighter cover (swooping in from `DIVE` 50 px above its lane; it was 70), departing helicopters (`HELI.OUT_Y` 126; they climbed to 80), the zeppelins (`ZEP.Y` 180, `TWIN_DY` 34) and dive bombers, which level off at their lane as they climb away. Smoke, a balloon floating off and the like drift where they will.

**The fighting stays on the page** (round 14; the sixth playtest saw "a decent amount of offstage combat", and a fifth of the planes the bots downed, a third of the balloons, still had their middle off the page, mostly to flak bursts and rockets reaching past the edge). Nothing is hit where you can't see it: shots off the page hit nothing (`inView`), a blast passes by a plane that doesn't show yet (`shows`, `canHit`), and the crew, the sentry and fighter cover only pick targets with their middle over the page. The Red Cross plane can't be hit until its middle is over the page. Holding every plane off until its middle was over the page was tried first: it gave each one a free half second and made the game clearly harder for the bots (decent bots alive at wave 20 fell from 34% to 17%). Bombers hold their bombs until they're over the page (`BOMB_EDGE`, 12 px in; 17% of their bombs used to come from off the page), so a mark near the edge is overshot a little. Even so, planes and cargo planes no longer going down before they show meant more bombs and tanks arriving: over 200 seeds the bots' bomb damage to the wall rose about a sixth and fewer reached wave 10. So a bomb on the wall does `BOMB_WALL` (16; it was 18), which brings waves 6 to 16 back to about where the sixth playtest left them.

The baseline crew fires slowly and aims at bodies in the lower sky. Bazookas go for tanks first and can target aircraft; engineers repair the wall (`REPAIR`: 4 HP/s for an engineer, 2 for anyone else pulled onto repairs). Wounded recruits have small health marks under their feet, and the squad row in the HUD shows health too (`miniFig`, drawn at `MINI` = 0.95 of field size): a bar under each figure, a slouch and bandage below 70%, and below 40% a further droop, a fade and a red cross. The row also shows each role's kit (rifle, hard hat, tube, scope, the medic's white helmet with a red cross). Rifle recruits learn to pop low chutes only after buying Catcher training.

All crew damage goes through `hurtRecruit`. Bombs wound by distance (a direct hit knocks a bare recruit down), landers punch, and snipers shoot. Two free supplies make the crew sturdier. **Dig in** (`S.mods.trench`, two stacks) cuts every kind of crew damage by 40%, then 60% (`TRENCH`), and draws sandbag rows in front of each crew group. **Sandbags** (+25 maximum wall health a stack, four stacks) are stacked against the front of the bunker, a layer a stack, five bags, then four, three and two on top (`BAGS`, `drawSandbags`); until round 14 they drew nothing (piled against its sides, as first tried, the crew standing there hid them). **Helmets** (`S.mods.helmet`, three stacks) add 1 health to current and future recruits (`crewMax`), and are drawn on everyone but engineers and medics. Dug in with helmets, a recruit survives a direct bomb hit on his feet.

Carpet bombers begin in wave 2 and release 3–6 bombs across the trench, crew positions and bunker. The whistle warns of a drop; bombs can be shot down. Snipers begin in wave 3, descend toward an edge, and fire at recruits from mostly beyond the barrel's reach. With no crew left, they shoot the turret instead (`sniperHitsTurret`): each hit adds 0.25 heat (which can tip a hot gun into overheating) and chips 3 wall. A red "sniper!" callout marks each one as he lands. Counters are the crew, mines, the sentry, a fully dipped shot, or squashing him with a popped trooper. A sniper with no crew to target leaves after `abandonAfter` (14 s) without awarding points, so he can't stall a wave.

## Title page and high scores

**The title is the page itself** (round 15, the polish pass: "it needs to be more engaging, especially in comparison to Thimbleful and Crack"). The hand-lettered logo sits across the top of the page with the tagline under it (and a keys line where there's a mouse), the game plays a short demo underneath, and Start, the record and the chips (Sound, Full screen, High scores) run along the bottom of the page, where the wall and squad usually are (`#titleScreen`, `.title-page`; the HUD's wall and squad rows aren't drawn on the title). The rules are pencil notes with arrows pointing at the demo: pop his chute low, over the mat; shoot the rest; caught ones join your squad and fight. The record is a red rubber stamp, "Best 446,661 / Won 2×", with the best wave once endless has gone past 20 (`bestLine`, `winLine`). The buttons around the page (mute, full screen) wait for play, since the chips do the same. Space or Enter starts a run unless a button has focus.

**The demo** (`DEMO`, `demoScene`, `updateDemo`) runs the real simulation (`update`) on the title with a scripted gunner: a plane crosses at `DEMO.Y` and drops two riflemen, one over the mat (`CATCH_X`) and one to the right (`SHOOT_X`). The turret swings round (`TURN` rad/s, leading the target for the 700 px/s bullets) and pops the first chute once he's below `CATCH_AT`, so he bounces into the squad and says "ready!"; it shoots the second below `SHOOT_AT`. A soldier calls out as the plane comes in (`DEMO.LINES`). After `REST` seconds with the page empty it starts over from the other side. The title squad is a rifleman and an engineer, so nobody shoots the plane. Nothing in the demo touches a run: `newGame` resets everything.

**On a wide screen the title opens the notebook** (`fit`, `SPREAD_ASPECT` 1.05, `#facing`): the game page on the left and a facing page on the right, with a shadow down the binding. Every button stays on the left page; the facing page is for reading: Orders from HQ on a yellow post-it (aim and hold to fire, in short bursts: the gun overheats; pop a chute low over the mat and he joins your squad; shoot the rest to protect the turret; spend dog tags on supplies between waves; defeat the Dreadnought; bonus: protect the Red Cross), the keys, and the online top five with a star for a win (`facingRows`, loaded only when the facing page shows). Start closes the notebook back to the single page, so play never changes. Phones in portrait keep the single page.

**High scores** (`board.js`, `BOARD` = 1, `scores/games.json`; see [the leaderboard guide](../guides/00-leaderboards.md)). The campaign is the score that counts (SPEC-008): a run is saved at the victory card when the Dreadnought goes down on wave 20, or at game over if it ended before that. A winner who keeps going plays endless for fun: its game over shows the board without asking again, since a run's token saves one row. Practice runs (a `#seed=`, the `#tune` panel) never ask for a token, so they can't be saved. Each row has initials, score, the wave reached with a star for a win (meta `wave`, `won`), and touch or keyboard (touch once a finger or pen touches the page in play; menus don't count). Ties go to the faster run (`time_ms`, which is `S.played`). The end card shows one step at a time, as Thimbleful does: "Checking the scores…" where its buttons go (at least `BEAT`, 0.6 s), then "New high score! You're #N" or "Save your run? You'd be #N of M" with Enter initials and Skip, then the picker with the card's buttons hidden, then the buttons back and the board below them; if the scores take longer than `WAIT` (2.5 s) the buttons come back and a late answer offers initials inside the board. The title's High scores chip opens the board on its own card (`#scoresScreen`; Escape closes it). Offline, nothing changes but the missing board.

**The cards** (round 15): the game-over and victory cards put their buttons right under the score, then the board, then the stats two to a line (shorter labels: Wave, Time, Captured, Popped, Planes, Zeppelins, Tanks, Red Cross, Damage, Rounds, Best), the squad and the fallen. Every card on the page scrolls when it's taller than the page (a full squad, a long roll call), so its buttons are always in reach; with a full squad the game-over card used to push Play again off a phone's screen.

**The score cap** (`plausible`) is 1,400 points a second plus 25,000. The expert bots' fastest run scored about 660 a second over a whole run (24 seeds, Oct 9), and the two owner wins about 470 and 385; the cap is about twice the bots' best, per the guide. The grace covers the biggest single moment (the Dreadnought going down with a combo, the captain caught, the last wave's bonus). Anything that makes points come faster starts a new board with a What's new note.

## Squad: names, ranks, the wounded and the field hospital

These rules live in `site/stick-army/squad.js` (`StickArmySquad`). Rookies are nameless. Standing at the end of a wave counts as a wave served (`serveWave`). Three waves earn a name and a stripe (Pfc.), six make Corporal, ten Sergeant, fourteen Staff Sergeant (SSgt.) and eighteen Master Sergeant (MSgt.), the top: five stripes (`RANKS`). Each stripe adds 0.5 health and an 8% quicker trigger (`RANK`); the bots showed that faster repairs on top made long-lived squads unbeatable.

**Training** (shop.js `TRAIN`, squad.js `train`) adds the same stripes. **Boot Camp** (60 tags, from the shop after wave 5) gives everyone in the squad a stripe now, the soldier in the tent too, and every soldier who joins after, hired or caught on the mat, starts with one (`makeRecruit`). **Elite Training** (150 tags, from wave 10, once Boot Camp is done) gives everyone one more stripe, not two, and new soldiers then start with two. Both are supplies in the shop's rotation like any other, war prices and all, and either can be the free one; there's no third level. A soldier's stripes are what he's served for plus what he's been trained (`stripes`: service stripes at 3, 6, 10, 14 and 18 waves, plus `r.trained`), capped at five, so the soldiers who've been there longest always have the most: a soldier from the first waves ends the run with five, a fresh hire after both trainings starts with two. A stripe from training brings a name and its health like any other. Special forces is kept back as a name for something unique later. The playtest ("could have like a boot camp, special forces, training") and the bots' wave-20 kit showed why: by then every supply is maxed with 4,000–5,000 tags unspent, and two thirds of the squad are rookie replacements. Names come from the run seed and the recruit's id, never from a game stream, so they can't change outcomes; they don't repeat within a run. Stripes show as yellow chevrons on the shoulder, in the field and in the squad row. Names appear only where there's room: a pop-up when a name or stripe is earned, the shop's squad news, the pause card (the squad drawn as in the HUD, then "Squad: Sgt. Doodle, Pfc. Inky, 2 rookies"; round 15 shortened it from waves and kills for each, which ran long on phones, and shows the kit as icons, as the shop does) and the game-over card's "Fallen" list (`S.fallen`). Each soldier keeps a kill count (`r.kills`): kills made by his own bullets and rockets are credited to him while they're resolved (`shooter`, `credit`). It shows on promotion ("Cpl. Inky! 85 kills"), in the shop news, on the pause card, in the fallen list and on the victory roll call (`SQUAD.record`).

At zero health a recruit falls wounded instead of dying (`knockDown`). He lies on the ground under a pulsing red cross and stops working. Any more damage finishes him: a bomb, a lander walking into him, a tank. Snipers aim past him at the next man standing, and their shots fly over him. A medic heals the wounded first and gets him back up at 1 health (`standUp`); a pizza does too.

The **field hospital** (60 tags, one stack) pitches a tent with one bed beside the right-hand crew (`TENT`, `drawTent`). When a wave clears, the bed goes to the most decorated wounded soldier, first down on a tie (`careAtWaveEnd`). He leaves the field, keeps his slot (`bedSlot`), shows in the squad row on a cot, sits out the next wave and rejoins at full health when it clears. Everyone else still down when a wave clears is lost, with or without the tent. The shop's squad news tells the story ("Cpl. Squiggle made it to the tent. A rookie didn't make it.").

## Drawn in

You're the commander drawing your army. Once the wave banner has been read (`SKETCH.DELAY`, 0.9 s), whatever you just bought is sketched onto the page in blue ballpoint, one piece at a time (`queueSketches`, `sketched`, `SKETCH`, 0.45 s each): the sentry tower and the tent from the ground up, the newest trench row, the newest layer of sandbags from the ground up, the wire and the second mat left to right, and new hires from their feet up. The pen (`drawPen`) has a steel tip, a blue grip, a clear barrel showing the ink tube, and a blue cap and clip, the same ink as your army. Called planes are sketched in at the left edge of the page before they fly (`STRIKE.HOLD`, `FIGHTER.HOLD`). It's purely a reveal: everything works from the first frame, and the sketch state never feeds the simulation. A pen-scratch sound marks each one.

## A readable page

Busy waves stack a lot on one page, so text and effects follow a few rules.

Floating text comes in kinds (`TEXT`, `addText`):
- **Alerts:** red threats ("sniper!", "rush!", "wall -18"). Bigger, held longer (1.3 s), always shown and drawn on top.
- **Story:** blue squad and radio news ("Pfc. Inky!", "man down!", "air strike!"). Always shown.
- **Big:** awards of 100 points or more ("tank down! +150"). Always shown.
- **Score:** routine kill labels. Small, soft grey and short (0.6 s). Kills of the same kind within 0.35 s and 44 px merge into one running label ("bonk! ×5 +250"); every trooper cry merges under the first one's word (`award`).
- **Minor:** little reactions ("clank!", "pop!", "+").

Score and minor labels share a budget of five on screen (`TEXT.BUDGET`); over it, new ones are skipped, and the combo counter and dog tags still show the reward. Wall damage from one burst is one running total (`wallText`). A new label steps a line up or down to clear one already there, and labels stay below the HUD band (`TEXT.TOP`). Dog tags from one burst fly to the counter as one bundle, and at most six bundles fly at once (`flyTags`). A long score shrinks to stop `HUD_GAP` (8 px) short of the wave label (`drawHUD`; round 14: six digits ran into "wave 10"), so seven-digit endless scores fit too.

A wave opens one thing at a time: a pizza ordered in the shop is delivered first (see Supplies and pizza), then the banner (`WAVE_BANNER`, 2.2 s), the sketches and the enemies. Once a wave's planes are done and the field is clear, a rush, cargo plane or zeppelin still due comes in after `WAVE_HURRY` (1.5 s; the zeppelin after its 2.5 s horn) instead of waiting out its timer, so there's no dead air. HQ's bomber at wave 9 and the zeppelin's reward are in their banners ("tanks! +1 air strike from HQ", "catch the crew! +1 air strike") instead of separate labels; `grantCall` takes a `quiet` flag for that.

**The highlighter** (`HL`) is the page's third pen, after your blue and their red, kept for your big moments so routine kills stay quiet. Downing a plane, tank or zeppelin, a mine and your air strike's bombs flash a comic yellow starburst (`pow`); a bomb hitting your ground does not. Labels worth `HL.PTS` (300) or more, and big awards, get a highlighter swipe behind them (`highlight`, drawn left to right in 0.14 s with `multiply`, so what's underneath shows through), and their soft grey turns to ink. Merged labels grow 1.5 px per kill, up to 9. Long labels are kept on the page by their width.

Past `FX.BUSY` (200) particles, effects thin out (`busy`): bursts throw a third of their flecks, every other puff is skipped, kills break into four pieces instead of six, midair spatter halves, and smoke trails behind falling planes thin from one puff every 0.07 s to every 0.14 s. All of it is cosmetic randomness, so it never changes outcomes. Ground ink also fades during a wave (see below).

## Rushers, tanks, armor and radio calls

These live in `site/stick-army/units.js` with the zeppelin. From wave 6 (`RUSH.WAVE`), rushes of two to six troopers charge in along the ground from one edge at `RUSH.SPEED` (48 px/s, about twice a walker's pace), with a whistle and a "rush!" callout (`spawnRush`). They're ordinary ground troopers once they arrive.

Tanks hit harder from round 13: a shell every `TANK.SHELL_EVERY` (3.2 s) for `TANK.SHELL_DAMAGE` (12), and a machine gun on the hull that fires bursts of `MG_BURST` (3) every `MG_EVERY` (2.6 s) at the nearest soldier standing in front of it within `MG_RANGE` (200 px; cause `tank`; `machineGun`), flashing at the muzzle. From `TANK.PAIR` (13) tanks by road come two at a time, one `PAIR_GAP` (74 px) behind the other ("tanks!"), and a tank stops behind another in its way rather than driving into it. From `TANK.ESCORT` (12) half of the road tanks come as a **tank raid** ("tank raid!", an enemy "advance!", event `tank_raid`): three to five infantry (`ESCORT_SIZE`, one more every three waves) walk in ahead of the tanks at `ESCORT_SPEED` (20 px/s, a little quicker than the tanks), ordinary ground troopers (`t.escort`) who go for the wall. The sixth playtest asked for tanks in pairs, stronger weapons and raids with troops.

From wave 9 (`TANK.WAVE`), cargo planes carry a tank (`spawnCargo`, `updateCargo`). Cargo planes are armored (`cargoHP`: 8, plus one every two waves after 9, with a health bar once hit). Down one before its drop and the tank goes with it ("tank and all!"). Otherwise the tank comes down under two pallet chutes at `TANK.FALL`, crated on its pallet so bullets only ping off it, flattens troopers it lands on, and rolls toward `TANK.STOP` (70 px from the wall, inside the barrel's dip), crushing a recruit in its way. It lobs a shell at the bunker every `TANK.SHELL_EVERY` (3.2 s), on the move as well as parked. Each shell does `TANK.SHELL_DAMAGE` (12) to the wall and wounds nearby crew. Shells fly like bombs and can be shot down, but they're faster and smaller (hit radius 6 instead of 9). Tanks shrug off bullets: each turret, rifle or sentry hit does `TANK.BULLET` (0.1). Rockets (4), mines and crashes (6) and air strike bombs (14) are the answer (`TANK.BLAST`, `blastTanks`). Health is `10 + 0.8 × wave` (`tankHP`), shown in a bar over the tank; a destroyed tank pays 150 points. From wave 11 (`TANK.ROAD_WAVE`), some tanks come by road instead (`spawnRoadTank`, `waveCfg` `road`): they roll in from one edge of the page with a red "tank!", stop at the same place, and only start shelling once they're on the page, so they can't all be stopped in the air. The first comes `ROAD.FIRST` (4 s) into the wave and the rest every `ROAD.GAP` (5–7 s, times the wave's pace), so they arrive while the planes and bombers are still overhead. Until the second win they came at 11 s and every 11–16 s: on wave 17 the last pair rolled in as the planes ran out, at about 27 s, and the wave dragged on to about 50 s against tanks alone ("they just need to be more impactful"). Round 16 ("a couple tanks straggling that kind of roll in, and you're like, what are you doing here, man?"): at 7 s and every 8–12 s they rolled out in time, but a tank takes about 13 s to drive in, so on wave 17 the last pair still arrived at about 31 s, after the planes (about 26 s); now it arrives at about 22–25 s. Mines and barbed wire work on tanks too. Other crew deal with ground troopers before tanks (`pickTarget`).

From wave 10 (`ARMOR.WAVE`), some troopers wear a grey flak vest that stops one body hit ("clang!", then "vest off!"). From wave 17 (`ARMOR.HEAVY`) the vest stops two and comes with a steel helmet. The chance is `armorChance` in `waveCfg`: 10% at wave 10, rising 4 points a wave to 60%. Snipers never wear one. A canopy hit still pops an armored trooper's chute and blasts kill outright, so the turret copes with armor better than the crew does (`armorHit`, `vest`, `steelPot`).

Radio calls are the player's specials. The radio holds `RADIO.SLOTS` (two) calls of either kind (`S.calls`, `callsHeld`):
- **Air strike** (`callStrike`, `updateStrike`, `STRIKE`): a friendly blue bomber crosses at `STRIKE.Y`, from the left or the right (`S.strike.dir`, picked with the combat stream so a replay is the same), and drops 11 bombs over the field as it goes, skipping the 46 px around the bunker. The blasts kill troopers and hit tanks hard but spare crew and the wall. 45 tags, sold from wave 3.
- **Fighter cover** (`callFighter`, `updateFighter`, `FIGHTER`): a flight of two small blue fighters, the wingman `WING_X` behind and `WING_Y` above the lead (`S.fighter.wing`), swoops down into its lane at y 190 (`FIGHTER.DIVE`, 70 px over `DIVE_T`, 0.5 s) and makes one pass across the sky at 250 px/s (`FIGHTER.PASSES`), from the left or the right like the air strike, trailing pencil contrails. Each plane strafes: a yellow tracer every 0.08 s, aimed at the nearest bomb, plane, Dreadnought gun or (last) zeppelin ahead of it (`fighterTarget`), or raking forward and a little down when nothing is, with big muzzle flashes and spent casings falling. It leaves the ground alone. 50 tags, sold from wave 3.

Every call goes out on the radio first (`radioCall`, `updateRadio`, `drawRadio`, `RADIO.TALK` = 0.6 s): the most decorated soldier standing, nearest the bunker on a tie, raises a buzzing walkie-talkie with the call's name over him and a burst of static. With no squad, the walkie-talkie appears over the bunker. Then the plane is sketched in and flies, with its engine sound.

A run starts with an empty radio. With the first tanks (wave 9), HQ puts a bomber on it (the banner says "tanks! +1 air strike from HQ"), and each zeppelin downed earns one (`grantCall`). A free call with the radio full pays 40 tags instead. The shop marks both calls "Radio full" when there's no room. One of each kind can fly at a time. Each button shows only while the radio holds its call, shows the count, and waits while that call is flying (`syncCallBtns`). The buttons beside pause are easy to miss on a desktop, so the calls held also show in the HUD under the score as small chips with the call's icon, its key (B or C, where there's a keyboard) and the count (`callChips`, `drawCallChips`, `CALL_CHIP`, 44 px wide so two clear the combo counter); a click or tap on one makes the call instead of firing (`callChipAt`), and a call already flying is greyed. They don't pulse or nag.

Rushers and tanks add variety mid-run. Pressure starts early and keeps rising through wave 19 and on into endless (`waveCfg`):
- **The low lane** (round 13, `LOW`): from wave 2 a share of the planes fly low across the middle of the page (y 246–296), and from wave 3 a share of the bombers (y 188–220), a little faster (`LOW.SPEED`, ×1.1). Their troopers have less sky to fall through and their bombs land sooner. The share (`low` in `waveCfg`) is 15% at wave 2, rising 3 points a wave to 40%; none on boss waves, whose escorts keep their own lanes. The first one says "flying low!". The sixth playtest asked for low drops and bombers early on.
- Planes number `5 + 2n` (7 at wave 1). Each drops up to `maxDrops` troopers: six by wave 8, seven from 10, eight from 12. The gap between them starts at 1.52 s and tightens to 0.98 s by wave 4, holds at 0.85 s from 5 to 9, then shrinks by 0.045 s a wave to 0.3 s by wave 21. ## Zeppelin boss

Every fifth wave (`BOSS_EVERY`) is a boss wave: a zeppelin at 5, the armored zeppelin at 10, two zeppelins at once at 15, and the Dreadnought at 20, the final wave (see below). In endless the twins come back on the fives and the Dreadnought on the tens. Zeppelin waves are announced as "zeppelin incoming!" ("two zeppelins incoming!"). `waveCfg` drops the bombers and halves the planes, and those escort planes keep to a high lane (`SKY_LANES.ESCORT`, y 120–130; 98–118 until round 14) above the zeppelin. A few escort planes come first. Once the wave's planes are done, another escort plane comes every `ZEP.ESCORT_EVERY` (2.4 s) while a zeppelin flies, from its own stream (`S.spawn.escort`), so a zeppelin is never alone on the page (the fifth playtest found the armored one lingering by itself). About 6.5 seconds in, a low horn and a red "zeppelin incoming!" give warning (`ZEP.WARN`), and the zeppelin enters from a random side at 9 seconds (`ZEP.ARRIVE`). It patrols between `ZEP.LEFT` and `ZEP.RIGHT` until it is shot down, so the wave can't clear while it flies. Turning is a cartoon flip: `p.face` eases through zero, so the hull squashes, stops and heads back. Its health is `20 + BOSS_HP_PER_WAVE × wave` (8 per wave, so 60 at wave 5), shown in a small red bar riding just above the hull, with a tick at half. The bar comes in with the hull as it enters, and is only held on the page once the zeppelin has arrived (`drawBossBar`). The gondola is the weak spot: direct shots there do `ZEP.WEAK` (double) damage (`inGondola`), with a "weak spot!" callout the first time and a banner hint ("zeppelin! aim for the gondola").

While on screen it drops a paratrooper from the gondola every 3.4 s and a three-bomb cluster from its belly every 6.5 s. Gondola drops are ordinary troopers, so they can be caught. Hits leave holes where they land (more as it weakens) that leak gas wisps, and it sinks toward the page as it loses health. At half health it turns angry: red-tinted, faster, dropping every 2.4 s and bombing every 4.5 s, with the horn again. The hull hit test is an ellipse narrowed while turning, plus the gondola (`planeHit`). It lives in `S.planes` as `kind: 'zeppelin'`, so flak, rockets, bazookas and the ambience treat it as an aircraft. Idle rifles also shoot at it when nothing closer needs them (`pickTarget`).

From wave 10 (`ZEP.ARMOR_WAVE`) the zeppelin comes armored ("armored zeppelin! strip its plates"): five riveted steel plates (`ZEP.PLATES`) cover the hull end to end, and a hit on one clangs and chips it instead of the hull (`armorTakes`, `plateAt`). Each plate takes `PLATE_HP` (6) before it falls away ("plate off!"), and the hull behind it is open. The gondola has a steel shutter, so the weak spot only opens ("gondola open!") when it turns angry at half health. Bazookas aim at its middle, so they strip the middle plate and keep hitting the hole.

**Two zeppelins** (wave 15, then 25, 35… in endless; `ZEP.TWIN_WAVE`, `waveCfg` `twin`): both arrive together from opposite sides ("two zeppelins at once!"), one `TWIN_DY` (34 px; 48 until round 14) above the usual height (`ZEP.Y`, 180; 172 until round 14) and one below, so the high one's health bar stays below the page's top rule, (`p.homeY`), each with `TWIN_HP` (60%) of a single zeppelin's health, both armored. Each has its own bar, and the wave lasts until both are down; each pays and bails out its crew as a single one does.

Shooting it down awards `250 + 30 × wave` points (with combo), a "zeppelin down!" banner ("catch the crew! +1 air strike", or "+40 tags" with the radio full), an air strike charge and its share of dog tags. Three crew bail out on chutes for last-chance catches. It then falls nose-first in flames, with small blasts along the hull, flattening other airborne troopers but not its own bailing crew, and crashes in three ground explosions.

## Supplies and pizza

The shop currency is **dog tags** (the balance is still `S.coins` in code). Kills, captures, planes, intercepted bombs, combos and wave completion earn tags separately from score. Every award spawns a tag that flies from where it was earned into the counter (`flyTags`, `TAG_HUD`); in a pile-up, new tags fold into one already in flight. The tag is drawn once into a cached sprite (`dogTag`). After all enemies, aircraft, bombs and hostile shots are gone, a short wave-clear banner leads to the shop. Combat is frozen there.

The shop is one priced list. **Supplies** shows three rotating supplies from one pool (`offer`, `OFFERS`), both radio calls from wave 3, and **Order a pizza** (25 tags). The field hospital is in the rotating pool. One of the three rotating supplies is on the house each visit: a random gift with an "On the house" badge, a struck-through price and "Free!" (`onHouse`, `costNow`). The gift row is otherwise drawn like the others, so it never looks already chosen. A bought supply keeps its ink and says "Packed ✓" in blue (`.bought`), with "tap to put back" under it: tapping it again returns it and the tags (`putBack`). An **Undo** link by the continue button puts back the last thing taken, hires and pizza included (`undo`). The shop keeps a snapshot from when it opened (the squad, the tent and the names in use included, so training put back takes its stripes and names with it) and the order things were taken (`S.shop.base`, `S.shop.log`); putting something back restores the snapshot and takes the rest again, so prices, the gift and hires stay consistent, and anything that only fit because of what went back (a hire into a returned slot) goes back too. Only rows you can't afford or use go grey. Training (Boot Camp, then Elite Training) joins the rotation when it opens (see the squad above). **Patch the wall** isn't offered while the wall is full (so it's never the gift then), and says "Wall is full", greyed, if the wall fills during the visit. **Hire** always lists every role, so the list never changes shape: rifleman 35, engineer 40, sniper 50, bazooka 55 and medic 55. What you can't hire is greyed. One medic at a time: with one in the squad he's greyed with "Have one" instead of a price. Whoever you hired this visit shows as a chip under the roles ("Hired: Rifleman ↩", `#hiredItems`); tapping one sends him back with the tags (`putBack`), and on a phone the row scrolls into view as you hire. Every hire, of any role, raises the next price by 15 (`price(item)` handles computed costs). **War prices** (`WAR`, `war`): after wave 6 supplies cost 15% more each wave (about three times by wave 19) and soldiers 22% more (about four times), rounded to fives, so the shop makes you choose late and a soldier caught on the mat is worth more. The supply note says so ("Supplies are running short: prices are up 144%"). Pizza stays 25 all war, and the gift is free. The fifth playtest had 6,500 tags in hand by wave 19 with nothing left to buy. Supplies sell once per visit; hiring repeats while slots and tags last. Unaffordable items say how many more tags they need on a line under the price, so rows keep their width as the balance changes; the continue button ("Wave N →") is always open, and tags carry between waves. A **Squad** section under Hire shows the squad as it stands (round 14: "show squad status on store"): every slot as in the HUD, empty ones dotted, with roles, stripes, health and whoever is in the tent (`drawSquadRow(canvas, true)`), and names, waves and kills (`squadLine(true)`), or "Nobody yet." It's redrawn as you hire, train or put things back. Your kit shows under the list as pencil icons with counts (`kitItems`, `renderKit`). Flak rounds burst only near planes and bombs, and the bursts spare paratroopers, even ones jumping from the plane that was hit. A direct hit on a trooper's body or canopy acts as a plain bullet, so catches still work with flak. Every supply and role has a pencil icon drawn with the battlefield pen in `icons.js` (`StickArmyIcons`, used through `ICONS` and `drawItemIcon`); add one when adding an item. Capped upgrades leave the pool, and repeatable repairs keep the rotation full in long runs.

Pizza is a nod to the owner's remembered delivery Easter egg. Ordering it marks the shop row "On its way" without leaving the shop (`S.pizzaOrder`). Before the next wave, a stick courier cycles onto the page, hands over a box and rides away (`updateDelivery`, `drawCourier`). It's its own scene (`S.waveState` `'pizza'`, `S.nextWave`): no enemies come until everyone is fed, and the wave starts as he rides off. At the handoff, the wall gains 25 health and every surviving recruit gains 1 health, capped at their maxima. Pizza does not resurrect fallen crew.

The mute button (`muteBtn`, saved as `stickarmy.muted`) shows a speaker, or a crossed-out speaker while muted (`updateMuteBtn`); the icons are SVG, so it sets their `hidden` attribute directly, since SVG elements have no `hidden` property (until round 12 the icon never changed, so the button looked broken). The shop hint warns what the next wave brings: a zeppelin, two zeppelins, the night raid or the Dreadnought (`bossHint`). The pause card shows your kit as icons with names (`renderKit`; repeatable buys are left out). **Wall damage taken** (`S.stats.wallDamage`, counted in `hurtWall`): the shop report shows the wave's ("wall -42 (86/100)", or "wall untouched"); a wave that never touched the wall pays half its wave bonus again and a tag a wave ("untouched! +950" in the wave-clear banner); and the run's total is on the game-over and victory cards and in play stats. Both end cards count **rounds fired** (`S.stats.shots`, round 14: "shots fired would be a fun stat"): every bullet and rocket from your turret, so the double barrel and spread shot make it climb (a trigger pull with both fires six). The game-over card names what brought the wall down, from the last source to hurt it (`OVER_CAUSE`: bombs, landers, snipers, tanks, the Dreadnought, a dive bomber, a balloon, helicopter fire or a heavy bomber's carpet), and adds zeppelin and tank lines once one has been destroyed (`S.stats.zeppelins`, `S.stats.tanks`). Under the stats it shows the squad still standing: their figures as in the HUD's squad row, roles and stripes (`drawSquadRow`), and their names, waves and kills (`squadLine`), then the fallen. The sixth playtest lost at wave 20 with a stacked squad the card didn't show. All equipment, tags and recruits reset on a new run. Only mute preference and the local best score persist. The changed rules use a fresh local-best key, `stickarmy.best.3`. Online leaderboards remain deferred by SPEC-002; this game has no `BOARD` or Worker changes.


**The flagpole** (round 16, Oct 9: "a flag pole that goes up... it boosts morale"; `FLAG`, `openWave`, `raiseFlag`, `drawFlagpole`, `drawFlagCloth`, `drawFallenFlag`): a supply in the rotating mix from wave `FLAG.FROM` (8; round 16: "the flag should not be available until wave 8"), 40 tags at war prices, once a run. The shop says only "It boosts morale." It does: the squad fires `FLAG.FIRE` (4%) faster while it flies. The first time, it goes up in its own scene before the next wave, after a pizza if one's coming (`S.waveState` 'flag'): the pole is sketched in with the flag at the bottom (`SKETCH`, 0.6 s), a beat (`PAUSE`), the flag is hoisted to the top (`HOIST`, 1.6 s; `S.flagUp` 0 to 1), and the squad salutes it (`SALUTE`, 1.4 s) while a soldier says "Look at her fly!"; then the wave starts. From then on the squad salutes it at every wave start (`S.saluteT`: the hand at the brow, the weapon at the side). The flag is blue pen with a white star, rippling in the wind, on a pole at the bunker's left back corner, flying left over the squad (`FLAG.DIR`), clear of the turret and the sentry gun. Round 16 moved it from the right back corner, where it flew over the sentry gun. Nothing targets it; at game over it lies across the rubble, and on the victory card it's still flying. ## Tuning and extending

`site/stick-army/game.js` contains the simulation and saved preferences. `site/stick-army/icons.js` holds the shop's supply icons: a classic script that loads before `game.js` and defines `StickArmyIcons(kit)`, which `game.js` calls once with its pen helpers and colors. Each icon is then drawn with the icon canvas context as `G`. `site/stick-army/units.js` holds the zeppelin, rushers, tanks and the radio calls; `sky.js` holds the Red Cross plane, bomb balloons, HQ crates, dive bombers and helicopters; `squad.js` holds names, ranks, kill counts, the wounded and the field hospital; `campaign.js` holds the Dreadnought, the victory card and endless; `shop.js` holds the item list, the shop, the kit display and the pizza courier. They are classic scripts that load after `icons.js` and before `game.js`, and define `StickArmyUnits(world)`, `StickArmySky(world)`, `StickArmySquad(world)`, `StickArmyCampaign(world)` and `StickArmyShop(world)`. `game.js` passes one `world` object of constants and helpers, with getters for live values (`S`, `G`, `boil`, `RW`, `RC`, `RS`, `sound`, `seed`), and keeps aliases (`spawnZeppelin`, `callStrike`, `openShop`, `ITEMS` and so on) so harnesses and `tune.js` reach them as before. `site/stick-army/audio.js` owns procedural sound through `StickArmySound.init()`, `.play(name)`, `.say(text, voice, enemy, delay)`, `.ambience(state)` and `.muted`; it loads before `game.js`. **Sound** (round 15, the polish pass, after an outside audio audit; picks made on a sound-check page). Everything is procedural, in `audio.js`. The mix runs at `LEVEL` (0.8, since phones played it quietly) into a gentle tanh soft clip; a compressor node was tried and squashed short hits. Effects and voices go through `fx`, which the title closes, so its demo plays silently under the title music and only the menu buttons (`click`, `press`, routed straight to the master) are heard; leaving the title opens it at once so the first bugle isn't swallowed. Muting fades the master out in a few hundredths of a second, so a long sound already playing stops too (it used to only stop new ones). The noise source is `NOISE_LEN` (3 s) and each use starts at a random point: it was 0.6 s, which cut the noise out of every longer sound (the Dreadnought's rumble and entrance, the heavy bomber's drone, the air strike, the zeppelin going down, the overheat hiss).

**Little voices** (`say`): gibberish chatter, a buzzy syllable for each vowel in the line (up to four) through that vowel's formants (`VOWELS`), so lines sound a little like themselves. Each speaker keeps his own pitch (from his id), the enemy lower and gruffer. A line ending in "!" lifts at the end. Lines don't talk over each other: one that would overlap a line already playing waits until it ends (up to `SAY.WAIT`, 0.6 s), except an alarm (mood `alarm`, passed through `world.say`: "medic!", "incoming!", "they're sneaking in!"), which plays at once, and small talk (mood `chat`), which is dropped; before, any line within 0.2 s of another was silenced, alarms included. Round 15 also gave each mood its own sound (alarms said twice, radio calls through a walkie-talkie, softer small talk); after a playtest ("everything was kind of different but similar enough") every line sounds the same again, and the moods only decide who gets through. The browser's own speech was tried in round 10 and dropped after the fourth playtest: the default voices sounded bad, and differently on every phone. The words are in the **speech bubbles**: the game speaks through `world.say` (`speak`), which puts a bubble with the line over the speaker, following him, or at a given spot (`S.bubbles`, `BUBBLE`, `drawBubbles`; blue for the squad, red for the enemy), and plays the chatter. Lines: the radio caller says the call ("Air strike!"), a soldier going down calls "Medic!", one helped up says "Thanks!", a new recruit "Ready!", a promotion "Yes sir!" (making Master Sergeant, "Master Sergeant of the page!"), a soldier marked by the Dreadnought "Incoming!", whoever fetches an HQ crate "Got it!", two of the squad cheer a cleared wave (`SQUAD.cheer`), the pizza courier announces himself, and the enemy yells as a rush charges and as a helicopter starts dropping. **Small talk** (round 14, the sixth playtest's easter eggs: "just more small talk"; `SQUAD.smallTalk`, `TALK`): when a wave goes quiet, with nothing on the page for `TALK.QUIET` (2.5 s), a soldier now and then says something ("Who keeps erasing my legs?", "I miss the margins"), and sometimes another answers ("Think they'll turn the page?" "Not on my watch"). At most one every 35–55 s, each line once until they've all been said. The night raid gets "Who turned off the lamp?" as it gets dark, and an overheated gun "Easy on the trigger!" now and then (`SQUAD.heat`, at most once a minute). It's cosmetic (`Math.random`, nothing reads it), and bubbles count their own ids (`bubbleId`), apart from `nextId`, so speech never shifts the ids of things in the fight. The balance adapter's sound stub has no `say`, so bots get bubbles but no chatter.

**Music.** The game calls `ambience` about every 80 ms with whether the title is showing, whether a wave is live, the wave number, the flying planes and whether the wall is below 30%. **The title** plays a short bugle march (`titleMusic`, `TUNE`, `TITLE.BPM` 116), original to the game and built on the four notes of the wave-start bugle call (G C E G), so the title, the wave start and the victory fanfare sound like one army. It builds in layers as it loops: the bugle and a snare; then oom-pah low brass and a bass drum; then a second bugle a third below, rolls and a glockenspiel; then back to the second layer and up again. The browser plays nothing until the page is touched, so it starts with the first touch or key on the title (`wakeSound`). **In play** a snare-and-bass-drum march runs while a wave does, scheduled slightly ahead of the audio clock (`marchStep`). It gets busier from waves 4 and 9 and speeds up a little as waves climb, It's the same march whatever the page is doing: round 15 had it thin out when the page was quiet and fill in when it was busy, which was dropped after a playtest ("there's a lot going on"). A zeppelin adds a low timpani on each downbeat. A low wall swaps the bass drum for a heartbeat in time with the march, with a soft knock higher up on each beat (`knock`), since a phone's speaker plays almost nothing under 100 Hz. The Dreadnought brings its own march (`dreadStep`); as it sinks and crashes the music stops and the crash carries it to the end of the wave (the wave's own march used to come back). Up to three propeller drones follow and pan with the nearest planes (not on the title). Everything is on one bus that fades out outside active play and when muted.

**Cues:** a bugle at wave start; the arpeggio at wave clear, and on the final wave only the cheer, since the victory card brings the fanfare; a shop jingle as it opens, the recruit ding for anything bought and a tink for putting it back; an overheat hiss, a ready ping when the gun unlocks, a squash; one whistle for every falling bomb (round 15 gave each kind its own: a slide whistle for balloons, a deep scream for heavy bombs, three whistles for a cluster; all were dropped after a playtest); a whistle for a rush, a cannon thump for tank shells, an engine roar and horn for the friendly bomber, a climbing whine for fighter cover, a burst of static and two beeps for a radio call, a rumble, a hissing flare, a heavy gun and a klaxon for the Dreadnought, four blasts of a klaxon ("a-OOO-gah", `honk`) for its alarms, a whine under quickening beeps as its main gun charges and a deep blast when it fires, a long crash with metal crumpling through it as its wreck hits the ground, a fanfare for victory, a dive bomber's howl as it tips over, a helicopter's chop, a heavy bomber's low drone, a chime for the Red Cross plane, coming in and getting across, and a sour buzz for hitting it, an engine and a little horn for HQ's supply plane, a pen scratch for each sketch, and for the zeppelin a horn, a soft canvas thup per hit and a groan going down. The paper tear (`rip`) was never used and is gone. `index.html` contains layout, the title/pause/end cards and the responsive HTML shop.

Double barrel and spread combine into six bullets per volley. Rockets add a separate projectile every fourth volley. Piercing normal bullets can hit three distinct targets; flak detonates once near an aircraft or bomb, consuming the round even when piercing is equipped, and against troopers it is a plain bullet. Mines rearm at the start of each wave and spare allies. Medics occupy a normal squad slot, heal other nearby recruits, and can be replaced in the shop after death. The sentry stands on a lattice tower just right of the bunker, clear of the main barrel. Every 0.7 s it fires an ordinary ally bullet at the nearest bomb or shell, then the nearest low chute, trooper on the ground, tank or low plane (`sentryTarget`, `SENTRY`). It does not inherit the player's weapon upgrades.

Corpses use six ink line/circle pieces (four on a busy page) with gravity, bounce and a two-second rest: red for enemies, blue for fallen recruits. Kills near the ground leave a faint splat; midair kills leave a spatter that fades in about a second, so the sky stays clean. Only one landed red fleck in three leaves a mark. During a wave the whole ink layer fades to 70% every four seconds (`DECAL`, `fadeInk`) in one compositing pass, without redrawing the marks, so the ground never builds into a solid band. Opening the shop washes the page (`washDecals`): every mark fades to 45% and the faintest go. New marks stamp directly onto the ink canvas once. Only the latest 500 are retained for replay on resize or the shop wash; older marks stay in the current raster until then or a new run. Reduced-motion preference disables screen shake and line boil.

## Seeds, events and balance bots

Randomness comes in streams ([SPEC-005](../../specs/SPEC-005-balance-bots.md)). `RW` (wave content: spawn timing, aircraft, drops, trooper types, the zeppelin) and `RS` (shop offers) are reseeded at every `startWave` from the run seed, so wave *n* is the same for a seed however earlier waves went. Each aircraft takes one draw from `RW` to seed its own sub-stream (`p.rng`) and pre-rolls its troopers (`p.kits`, from `rollTrooper`), so shooting it early doesn't change who jumps or what follows. `RC` covers combat outcomes (crew aim and timing). `R` and `rr` are cosmetic only and use `Math.random`. Shop offers walk a seeded shuffle of each tier and take the first eligible items (`offer`), so they change only when eligibility does. `newGame` picks a random seed unless `RUN.force` (harnesses) or `#seed=N` sets one; the hash is `&`-separated tokens (`hashTokens`), so `#tune&seed=42` works.

`emit(type, data)` does nothing in normal play; harnesses set `emitHook`. Events: `wave_start`, `wave_clear` (`untouched`), `plane_spawn` (with drops and trooper types), `trooper_spawn`, `kill` (`by` player, crew, explosion, crash, squash, strike; explosions add `source`), `chute_pop` (`overMat`), `capture`, `rip`, `splat`, `land`, `recruit_down` (`cause`, `rank`), `recruit_revived` (`by` medic or pizza), `recruit_saved` and `recruit_back` (the tent), `recruit_lost` (`cause` bomb, lander, sniper, tank, heli; `wounds` when lost at the end of a wave), `rank_up` (`rank`), `wall_damage` and `wall_repair` (`source`, `amount`; wall changes go through `hurtWall` and `repairWall`), `bomb_dropped` (`by` bomber, tank, zeppelin, balloon or diver), `bomb_intercepted`, `plane_down`, `coins` (`amount`, `reason`), `shop_offer` (`items`, `gift`), `purchase` (`item`, `tier`, `cost`, `gift`), `pizza`, `rush` (`side`, `count`), `tank_drop` (`hp`), `tank_down` (`by`), `air_strike` and `fighter_cover` (`left`), `armor_hit` (`by`, `left`), `zeppelin_warning`, `zeppelin_plate` (`left`) and `zeppelin_open`, `medevac` and `redcross_hit` (`lost`), `redcross_safe` (`tags`), `crate_drop` (`kind`), `crate_land`, `crate_caught` (`kind`, `by` crew or none), `refund` (`item`, a put-back in the shop), `dive`, `rope`, the Dreadnought's `dread_arrive` (at its first station), `dread_mark` and `dread_hit` (`target`), `dread_saved`, `dread_gun` (`by`, `left`), `dread_tease`, `dread_hangar`, `dread_launch`, `dread_bail` (`n`), `dread_bridge`, `dread_charge`, `dread_cannon` (`wall`), `dread_cannon_saved` (`by`), `dread_cannon_down` (`by`), `dread_board`, `dread_crash`, `surrender`, `victory` (`wave`, `score`) and `game_over` (`cause` is the last source to hurt the wall).

The balance adapter (`tests/stick-army/balance.js`) stops the page's frame loop and sound. In fast mode (the default) it also skips cosmetic effects (`addDecal`, `puff`, `burst`, `pow`, `killFx`, `addText`, `flyTags`); runs with effects on restore them. The bot (`tests/stick-army/bot.js`) ranks bombs bound for the bunker or crew, then chutes over a mat (it aims at the canopy's outer edge to capture), troopers about to land or at the wall (as low as the barrel dips), aircraft, then other chutes. It ranks bombers above troopers, shoots tanks on the way down or parked (as low as the barrel dips) and cargo planes, calls an air strike when a tank is parked, five or more troopers are on the ground, or the wall is below 30% (casual players only below 25%), and calls fighter cover when two bombers or four bombs fill the sky. Balloons it pops only while they're out over the field (x < 100 or > 300); a dive bomber in its dive comes before nearly everything, a heavy bomber just above bombers, a helicopter just after them, and troopers on a rope like low chutes. It leaves tanks alone while they're on their chutes, and decent and expert bots hold fire while the Red Cross plane is in the line of fire (`clear`; casual bots don't check). It sees the Dreadnought only through its parts (`o.dread`): the gun that's aiming comes before anything, then the open hangar or the exposed bridge, then its other guns. A gun starting to aim has to be noticed like a new target (each aim has its own tag), as a player would, instead of the bot switching to it the instant it starts. A win ends a bot run like a loss does (the adapter's `status().over` includes `won`), and the report counts wins. Its shop is one function: take the gift, hire a rifleman if the squad is down to one or none, buy the top four of its priority list (air strike, spread, double barrel, fighter cover), holding one call before wave 8 and two after, and the field hospital once it has four crew, hire up to two by the role the squad lacks most (bazooka, engineer, medic, then rifle; the adapter passes the war-priced step between hires, `hireStep`), buy the rest of the list with what's left (experts keep a 40-tag cushion), and order pizza when the wall is low. Run it with `python3 tools/balance/run.py stick-army`; the [balance bots guide](../guides/01-balance-bots.md) covers flags, profiles and the report.

| Skill | Median wave (quartiles) | Alive at wave 8 / 10 / 15 | How runs end |

What the bots found:

- **Flak spoiled captures and then blocked trooper kills.** A flak round that hit a canopy burst harmlessly above the body, and bursts near planes killed troopers as they jumped. Both are fixed: bursts spare paratroopers, and direct hits act as bullets. With the old behaviour, decent and expert medians were 8 and 7.5 waves; they are now 9 and 46.
- **Runs split in two.** Most runs end between waves 7 and 9, mostly to bombs, but runs that survive that stretch tend to last until the time cap. Pressure stops rising around wave 13: the spawn interval bottoms out at wave 7 and fall speed at wave 13, and later waves only get longer. A build whose kill rate beats the spawn rate (flak, spread, double barrel, the sentry) survives indefinitely. Expert runs that bought flak reached a median of wave 47; those that didn't, about 6 (correlation, not cause).
- **A lander can stall a wave.** With only an engineer and a medic left, one lander at the wall matched the engineer's repairs (then 6 HP/s each way) and the wave never ends unless the player dips the barrel to shoot him.

## Play stats

Each run reports to [play stats](../guides/03-play-stats.md) from `newGame` and at `showOver`, through `runReport`; a restart from pause reports the old run as quit. A run's online-board token goes with it (`token`, `board` 1), so a saved run can be matched to its row; `input` is touch or keys, as on the board. The score is `S.score` and the time is `S.played`. `stats` are `wave`, `kills`, `captured`, `popped`, `planes`, `zeppelins`, `tanks`, `wall_damage` (the run's total), `shots` (rounds fired, `S.stats.shots`), `captain` (the Dreadnought captain's fate, once he's out), `crew` (alive), `fallen`, `tags` (dog tags in hand), and `cause` (what brought the wall down) at game over. A win reports as soon as the victory card shows (`reportWin`, called with `showWin`), with `won_at`. **Keep going** resumes the same run, so it reports again if the page is hidden mid-run and at the final game over, with `endless`; it stays won. Harness and bot runs report nothing (automated browsers are skipped).

Canvas resolution adapts to the device (see below).

Measured in headless Chromium on this workspace (15-second stress sample, 4× CPU throttle):

| Version | Mean RAF / FPS | RAF p95 / p99 | Mean loop CPU / p95 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Before fixes (`1e42859`) | 92.02 ms / 10.9 | 166.6 / 216.7 ms | 43.28 / 82.1 ms |
| Direct stamping + phone resolution cap | 32.71 ms / 30.6 | 66.7 / 100 ms | 6.54 / 11.8 ms |

Canvas resolution now follows the screen's pixel ratio, up to 2 on touch screens and 2.5 elsewhere (`renderCap`). If play averages slower than 24 ms a frame over two seconds, it steps down half a ratio at a time to a floor of 1 and never steps back up (`noteFrame`), so capable phones get sharp art and weak ones keep the old frame rate. Headless Chromium rasterizes in software, so under the 4× throttle it steps down to 1 during warm-up and then matches the numbers above; `perf.py` reports the scale it ended on (`canvas_px_per_logical_px`). The ink regression is fixed, but this throttled stress workload still misses 60 FPS. These are measured RAF intervals, not an FPS estimate from JavaScript execution time. Treat phone performance as an open playtest issue before featuring the game; the harness makes further renderer work reproducible.
